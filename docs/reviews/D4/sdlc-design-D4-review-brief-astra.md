## What D4 is

The design that lets the engine act on the world once and know what it did. Its parts: a **deployment adapter contract** of two effects and four reads, each bounded, whose receipts are claims and whose only outcome source is a `reconcile` read of the target's own state (§2); **artifact and configuration identity**: an artifact projected from the object store and sealed under a digest of its canonical manifest, provenance in separate immutable mappings, owner-written immutable configuration versions whose identity includes keyed digests of the secrets, and an **identity read** that binds the running application process by a chain of host-side facts and hashes the tree it runs from through `/proc` (§3); the **deploy operation**: an engine-derived authorization consumed into a journaled operation under an environment lease, attempts with a per-environment generation, preconditions reread before the effect, a kill matrix, retry only after reconcile, an environment-wide generation guard, and a teardown that touches only what it positively owns (§4); **verification by observation**: environment-bound checks on D3's runner reaching the service only through an engine-brokered link, bracketed by two identity reads, one `deployment_verifications` row, `alpha_complete` and Alpha Deployed (§5); the **environment record** with its three facts, the observation job and out-of-band detection with `teardown` or `acknowledge` and no `adopt` (§6); **secrets** delivered on the launch authorization's reply and never through a unit property, an argument, a record or an agent (§7); the **Release Operator** as engine code with no model (§8); the first target **`local_service`**, a transient user service running D2's launcher with a new `service` profile, named from the engine home's hash, read only through the engine (§9); the carried items (§10); what it does not claim (§11); nine proposed corrections J1 to J9 and ten open questions Q1 to Q10 (§12); closed enumerations (Appendix A); the adapter interface (Appendix B); and 48 test statements in five lanes (Appendix C).

Facts about how it was written. It was revised once for six findings, RV1 to RV6, from a review Sean passed before yours; the revision is in the draft, cited where it applies, and does not stand in for your review. It rests on two probes on this host with stand-ins (preamble, §9.6, §11 class B): D2's launcher, init, constructed root and launch authorization were in neither. It departs from its brief in three places and says so: environment-bound checks reach a local target through an engine-brokered service link rather than D2's proxy (J8, where brief V1 said the proxy); service domains are exempt from D2 §3.3's restart closure (J2, where the brief said D2's isolation applies wherever D4 runs a process); and it is longer than the brief asked (D3's length or shorter), which draft 2 will reduce editorially. Its Release Operator answer (R1, engine code) is written as a recommendation adopted throughout the text.

## Where to press hardest

- §3.4 and RV1: the application process is found by the init's reported namespace pid, resolved to the one cgroup member with that innermost `NSpid` whose parent is the recorded init. An application can fork a child that executes the same runtime with the same arguments and then exit; the orphan is reparented to the domain init (namespace pid 1). Does the chain then read `differs` or `unread` at every later read, as §3.4 says, or can the first read at t0 (§5.3) bind the child? What does the init report, and what does the observation job record, when the application exits and a descendant keeps the port open? D3 L4's orphan rule is a check's; D4 states none for a service.
- §9.2 and J2: a service domain outlives the engine, and recovery never closes it. The init's control channel is "a socket the engine reconnects to after a restart". What authority does a new incarnation have over an init a dead incarnation launched, what can a same-uid process do with that socket meanwhile, and what happens to the running service when the store that recorded it is lost or restored from a backup (§4.6 leaves an unowned unit running and records it out of band; is "left alone until a hand `systemctl --user stop`" an acceptable end state)? Is the transient unit's restart policy stated anywhere; what does a manager-driven restart of a launcher whose launch state is spent do?
- §2.4 and §9.2: "before any reconcile read after a restart, the engine closes the attempt's launch". Between the new incarnation's start and that closure, can a launcher of the old incarnation still be granted (the socket path is the same), and does the capability's incarnation check (§2.2) close it? For the `late launcher` qualification case (§2.6): does "`reconcile` answers `absent` once the unit has ended" leave a window where the unit exists, has run nothing, and the attempt is read `partial` and sent to `rollout_partial` for a non-event?
- §4.5, J9 and RV2: the environment lease is held from intent through verification and completion. A verification writes no row "until the full set exists"; an execution quarantined with no row (D3 §2.6) or a check `missing` past `check_infra_retries_max` ends in `unknown`, but what bounds the time to that `unknown`, and can the lease be held indefinitely with the preempting teardown (RV4) as the only exit? Is `abandon` reachable from an operation stuck in verification?
- §5.2: the service link gives the service domain an ingress D2's domains never had (the init forwards a socket into the network namespace). Can a check reach anything but the frozen generation's socket; can the service reach the check back through it; what does the relay do with a connection in flight when the stop step of the next deploy terminates that generation; and does the link's log, through the redactor, record enough to show which generation answered?
- §5.3 and J7: the two identity reads bracket the checks with "one instance throughout". The observation job (§6.2) runs meanwhile and may make its own identity read; an out-of-band observation between t0 and t1 makes the row not `verified`. Can a false out-of-band detection (a read past `adapter_read_deadline` on a loaded host, E87 item 7) make verification fail or stay `unknown` so often that the journey is not repeatable, and is `unknown` then the right outcome?
- §2.6 and §11 class B: class B says its items stand "until the adapter's qualification establishes" them, but the qualification table has no case that kills an engine incarnation, restarts the engine between launch and read, or uses `pivot_root` as such; those are sandbox and kernel rows of Appendix C (D4-O03, D4-O04, D4-T01). Which class B items does §2.6 actually establish on an operator's host, which only the test lanes establish on this host, and should §11 say so?
- §3.1 and RV3: the artifact excludes `.surety/` and the configured paths, refuses links and special files, and carries modes in its manifest. For a real Node service with no build step: does "tracked files minus `.surety/`" include what the start command needs and nothing a check must not see; is a tracked file whose mode differs between the object store and the sealed copy a `differs` at the read; and can two mappings of one artifact (RV3) let a superseded candidate's verification be credited to the wrong revision anywhere in §4.1, §5.3 or §5.5?
- §3.2 and RV5: the configuration's identity includes HMAC digests of the secret values, and a value change is detected only at an engine start. Is "a file replaced while the engine runs changes nothing" consistent with F §10's rotation, and does `secrets_changed` leave the running service with the old value for an unbounded time without the record saying so?
- §7.2: secrets travel on the launch authorization's reply to the domain init and land in the service process's environment. From the host, the same uid can read `/proc/<pid>/environ`; the identity read records `argv`; `logs` captures the service's output through the redactor. Is any engine read or record able to carry a value (not a reference) into the store, and is §11 class C's statement of the reach complete?
- §4.6 and RV4: positive ownership is store intent plus the service manager's `ControlGroup` equal to the recorded domain cgroup. Across a user-manager restart or a changed `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, cgroup paths or bus addresses can change: does ownership then fail closed (nothing stopped, everything out of band) in a way the operator can recover from without `--all`, a pattern, or touching the manager?
- §4.1, J3 and J4: outside harness mode the route derives the digest, identity and targets itself, and `alpha_authorize` gains obligations. Which accepted M1 and M2 rows (SEAM §75's fixtures, the M2 real-lane project) change, and is the harness-mode split confined as SEAM §7 requires?
- §6.3 and J6: no `adopt` for an environment, only `teardown` or `acknowledge`. After `acknowledge` the environment shows `degraded` "until a later engine operation replaces what runs": is an Alpha environment someone restarted by hand then stuck `degraded` with a verified deployment nobody can re-verify, and should re-verification of the current attempt (§5.3's `POST …/verify`) be allowed to clear it?
