# D6: the decisions waiting for Sean

**Status:** prepared by the D6 designer, 2026-10-12, from D6 draft 1 (`sdlc-design-D6-idea-to-alpha.md`), the foundations, errata E1 to E137 and the real lanes' records. **Not decided.** Each question is one the design could not settle by reading the foundations; everything it could settle is in D6 §12.2 as a reading Sean may overrule. Recommendation first, hidden costs said plainly. Each decision, once made, is recorded in the errata as decided by Sean.

**What D6 is, in one paragraph.** Surety can take a hand-entered approved baseline through stages to a verified Alpha deployment, one stage at a time with a human `continue` after every role. D6 designs the part before that and the part that joins it up: **three conversational roles as engine sessions** (Vision, Spec Writer, Architect), each turn a metered one-shot invocation with the conversation in its context package, each product committed by the engine and approved by the owner; **the specification as a governed artifact** with a change request, a Spec Writer's proposed diff on a side ref, an exact impact analysis, the owner's approval, and invalidation that flows to checks, stages and gates; **planning as the foundations describe it**: a roadmap for the whole project, a mechanically checked plan for the next phase only, the Architect re-entering after each phase from durable records; and **the engine driving the whole build**: checks authored per phase before any stage, stages ordered by their dependencies and pipelined, an autonomy profile that runs a phase unattended within the floor, the phase gate, replanning, and a policy that requests the Alpha deployment D4 already performs. It states where a Surety-run build's time and tokens go, and the read that measures them for the benchmark after M5 (E130 item 9).

## Part 1. The nine open questions in D6 (§12.3)

| # | In plain terms | Options | Recommendation and cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | How does a conversation continue from one turn to the next? | (a) the engine holds the conversation: each turn is a one-shot invocation whose context package carries the transcript so far and the artifact's current state; no provider session is resumed; (b) D2 §1.8's design: the provider's own session files are carried between turns as a record and restored, and the backend is asked to resume them | **(a)**. ## Part 2. The corrections D6 proposes to D1, D2, D3, D4 and the engine as built (§12.1)

These change rules already built or approved; each names its tests in D6 Appendix C. **Recommendation: approve all nine.** The ones that change what the kernel does today are marked.

- **K1.** *Changes the kernel.* A transition that makes work eligible, ends a run, records a result, finalizes an operation or answers a decision requests a tick, so the next tick follows at once instead of up to `tick_interval` (30 s) later. One tick at a time, as now. (Removes up to minutes of idle per stage.)
- **K2.** *Changes the kernel.* Project `max_concurrent_runs` may exceed 1 (default 2), under one run per work item, serial integration and a session admitting no one-shot beside it; a project override of `domain_memory_max`, never above the engine's. (Q4.)
- **K3.** *Changes the kernel.* `max_chained_roles` stops meaning "a human after N roles" and becomes a hard bound (default 24); the project policy `autonomy: step | stage | phase` decides where the boundary falls. (Q3.)
- **K4.** *Changes the kernel.* `registerPlan` takes a whole phase plan (phase number, prepared-against versions, stages with tasks, dependencies, validation scope and integration obligations), registers `check_authoring` first and makes stage work depend on it and on its stages; the integration finalizer runs the mechanical check and routes; the fixture becomes one labelled caller of the production function.
- **K5.** *Changes the kernel.* The `phase` gate is built (D3 defined its scope and left it refused); `phase_verification` work; a `phase` sign-off scope.
- **K6.** *Changes the kernel.* Sessions as Q1 (a): the refused routes lifted; `session_qualified` from a continuation canary on a one-shot entry; D2's `session_headless` mode stays reserved; a `session_transcripts` record.
- **K7.** `check_authoring` is a new work kind; `replan` and `spec_change` gain the runs D6 §5.6 and §4.3 give them.
- **K8.** Side refs `refs/surety/spec-change/<seq>` for proposed spec diffs; `RequirementStatus` `withdrawn`; the change request and impact records.
- **K9.** The policy `alpha_deploy` (Q5); D4's Q3 default kept.

## Part 3. Choices D6 made that you should see (not in its question list)

- **The first phase plan is approved with the architecture, in one consolidated item** (§3.4; §12.2 reading 2): F §3.3's Developing gate names the task plan, so no act is added; later plans follow F §3.10.6 and need you only when they change goals, ADRs or the spec, or say they are unsure. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **A spec may be approved with open questions**, unless one blocks a requirement the roadmap hint places in phase 1 (§12.2 reading 1), reconciling F §3.2's "blocking questions resolved" with the template's §10. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **Vision is optional:** `surety project create --idea` records the idea you wrote (F §3.2 "human creates"); the Vision session is there when you want the conversation (§12.2 reading 5). **Recommendation: accept.**
- **Checks are authored per phase, before any stage, against the plan's declared interfaces** (§6.2; F §3.10.3, §5.1); checks that cannot be written until an interface exists are deferred by name and added after the stage that creates it. The Verifier's package carries the module map's interfaces, which is what E103's and E135's first-try defects lacked. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **No evidence reuse across a spec change** (§4.5): the runner re-runs, which costs no model; F §3.8's applicability assessment is not built. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **A stage is eligible when its dependencies are integrated, not verified** (§6.4): this is what lets stage n+1 build while stage n is verified; a stage-n fix lands by rebase and its candidate supersedes. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **The pace read is a projection from rows that exist** (§7.6), with `unknown` where a row is missing; nothing is built for the benchmark alone. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **What D6 does not claim** (§11), worth knowing: the mechanical plan check proves references, approvals, dependencies and coverage, never that a plan is sensible; a `human-test` requirement is never `verified` by a gate and stays visibly pending; the redactor catches registered forms only, so a secret typed into a session in another form reaches the provider and the transcript record; §7.2's times come from one-file stages in the real lanes and are not a prediction for a real stage.
