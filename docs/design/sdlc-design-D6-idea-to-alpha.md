## 1. The journey

What M5 must prove first, end to end, on a scripted backend and then once for real (E40). One project, from an idea the owner types to `alpha_deployed`. Each step names the human act, if any, and the engine record it leaves; the sections after say why each is safe. The project is T2 with two phases of two stages, the smallest shape that exercises a phase boundary and the Architect's re-entry.

1. **The owner creates the project with the idea:** `surety project create <name> --repo <path> --idea idea-brief.md` (IBT's form, or a paragraph). The engine bootstraps the repository as D1 §3.1 (journaled `.surety/project.json`), commits `.surety/idea.md` as the idea record version 1, and records the baseline `idea` (F §3.2: "human creates or accepts the idea"). `project.created`, `project.registered`, `baseline.idea_recorded`. No model.
2. **A Vision session** (§3.1), opened by `surety session open vision`: the owner and the role talk; each turn is one metered invocation (E10); the role rewrites `.surety/idea.md` into the project identity (§3.1); `surety session save` commits it as an `intent` revision; `surety session close` ends the run and raises `idea_accept` with the record as evidence. The owner accepts: idea record version 2, still baseline `idea`. **Optional:** an owner who wrote the brief himself skips this session (the human created the idea, F §3.2).
3. **A Spec Writer session** (§3.2): the role turns the idea record into `.surety/spec/spec.md` in TPL's shape; at every save the engine parses TPL §5's index (D3 §4.5) and tells the session what did not parse; close raises `spec_approval` with the parsed index, the open questions and the sensitive areas as observed evidence. **The owner approves** (F §9.1's floor): `spec_revisions` version 1, `requirements` registered with criteria and areas, `modules` from TPL §6 with their sensitive areas, the environments of TPL §7 recorded by name, baseline `spec_ready`. `baseline.spec_approved`.
4. **An Architect session** (§3.3): from the approved spec the role writes `.surety/architecture/architecture.md` and `modules.json`, `.surety/adrs/ADR-nnn.md`, `.surety/roadmap/roadmap.md` and `roadmap.json` (both phases, their goals, modules, requirements and dependencies), and `.surety/phases/phase-1.md` and `phase-1.json` (two stages with tasks, dependencies and validation scope). At close the engine runs the mechanical plan check (F §3.10.6; §5.3) and raises `architecture_approval` consolidated with `plan_approval` for phase 1 (F §3.3: the Developing entry gate is "ADRs, task plan, and validation scope; architecture approved"). **The owner approves.** `architecture_revisions` 1, `modules` refined, `roadmap_revisions` 1, `phase_plans` 1 registered with its two `stages` and their `stage_build` work (`registerPlan`, as built, extended by K4). `baseline.architecture_approved`, `baseline.roadmap_revised`, `baseline.plan_approved`. The candidate lifecycle is now `developing` (F §3.3).
5. **Phase 1 opens with the Verifier's check authoring** (§6.2; F §3.10.3, §5.1): `check_authoring` work, one Verifier run, writes under `.surety/checks/` the definitions and programs for every criterion of phase 1's requirements, the `smoke` and `security_lint` kinds the tier needs (D3 §4.3), the floor checks of every sensitive area touched, and the `post_deploy_identity` and `post_deploy_behavior` checks TPL §7 specifies (D4 J4). The diff is captured as a protected proposal (D1 §7.3 step 2), classified `tightening` (D3 §3.1: checks added with valid definitions), applied by a Reviewer run under `classifier_authority` `authoritative` or by the human under `recommend` (D3 §3.3). `protected.applied`; protected version 2 effective. Before anything is built, the checks exist and fail.
6. **Stage 1** (§6.3): the engine dispatches a Builder (`stage_build` eligible, its dependencies none); the Builder builds in a sandboxed workspace, writes its result, the engine snapshots, validates and integrates (D1 §7.3, §7.5), nominates candidate c-1 (T2 cadence, F §5.6, E11), registers and runs its checks (D3 §2.5, no model), dispatches the Verifier (`verification` work) and then the Reviewer (`review` work), evaluates the `stage` gate, and on `satisfied` completes the stage's work. A failed check sends the Builder back (D3 §2.10); a finding dispositioned `fix` makes fix work (E43). **No human act** within the stage under the phase autonomy profile (§6.5), unless the foundations route one: a loosening or unclassifiable check correction, a Medium deferral, a blocker.
7. **Stage 2** follows on stage 1's `integrated_revision` (its `depends_on`); its Builder may start while stage 1's Verifier and Reviewer run (§6.4). Records as step 6, candidate c-2 superseding c-1 (D1 §3.3, as built).
8. **Phase verification** (§5.8; F §3.10.4): with both stages verified, the engine nominates the phase candidate if the cadence has not, evaluates the `phase` gate for phase 1 (its scope every requirement assigned to the phase, D3 §4.2), and dispatches `phase_verification` work for the Verifier (regressions in what the phase touched). Satisfied: `phase_plans.completed_at`, `baseline.phase_complete`. Phase completion establishes nothing about deployment (F §3.10.4).
9. **The Architect's re-entry** (§5.6; F §3.10.5): `replan` work, one Architect one-shot run with a context package built from durable records, never from the first session; it commits `phase-2.json` and `.md`, perhaps a roadmap revision; the finalizer runs the mechanical check and, the plan staying within the approved baseline and architecture, registers phase 2 with no human act (F §3.10.6). A plan that changes a phase goal, alters an ADR, needs a spec change or declares itself uncertain raises `plan_approval` instead.
10. **Phase 2** repeats steps 5 to 8 for its two stages; its check authoring adds the phase's checks as a tightening.
11. **A specification change, mid-build** (§4; F §3.8), because a real project has one: the owner submits `surety spec change-request --file cr-1.md`; a Spec Writer run writes the proposed diff on a side ref; the engine computes the impact (affected criteria, checks, ADRs, stages, evidence); `spec_change` is raised with the impact visible; **the owner approves** (the floor, as spec approval); spec version 2 is committed, the affected results invalidated, `check_correction` work registered for the Verifier, and early replanning (F §3.10.7) registered for the Architect when the change touches the current or a later phase.
12. **Alpha** (D4 §1): the owner configured the environment and qualified the adapter once (D4 steps 1 and 2; both can happen any time before this). With the last phase complete, the deployment request is made by the owner's command (D4 Q3) or by the engine under the project policy `alpha_deploy: on_phase_complete` (§6.8, decision Q5); the engine seals the artifact, evaluates `alpha_authorize` over the whole delivered requirement set, deploys, verifies by observation, and on `alpha_complete` moves the candidate `developing → alpha_deployed` (`candidate.advanced`). Requirements no phase delivered stay visibly pending (F §3.10.2, §3.10.4).

**Human acts the journey requires:** the project creation (1), the spec approval (3), the architecture and first-plan approval (4), the spec change approval (11) and, unless policy says otherwise, the deploy command (12). Four to five, and every one is on F §9.1's floor or on F §3.3's entry gate. The Vision session and the owner's conversation in steps 2 to 4 are human time by choice, not ceremony. **Everything else is the engine's, and no agent approves anything** (E12: no role emits a gate outcome).

## 2. Sessions: how a conversational role runs

**2.1 A session is a run of kind `session`** (E10; D1 §2.6, §3.2, §4.2, §15.2; A.2 `RunKind`, `SessionState`). It holds one role, one capability grant, one budget, one workspace and one run lease, and a sequence of **turns**, each its own invocation receipt, execution domain and ledger row (D1 §13.1: "Session finalization reconciles turn invocations and creates no row for the session itself"). The session states and transitions are D1 A.5's, unchanged: `allocated → turn_running ⇄ open_idle → saving → open_idle`, `open_idle | saving → closing → finalizing → ended`. Opening a session starts no process (D1 §15.2); an idle session owns no process, because its turn's domain was terminated with closure before `open_idle` was recorded (D2 §1.8, §3.2, §4.4).

**2.2 A turn is a one-shot invocation with the conversation in its context package** (decision Q1, recommended (a)). The owner's message is written by the engine, verbatim and redacted (D1 §14.2), as the next entry of the session's **durable transcript** (§2.3) in the same transaction that moves the session `open_idle → turn_running`. The engine then builds the turn's context package (D2 §1.3): the role prompt (§3), the session's transcript so far, the current state of the role's artifact in the workspace (the workspace itself is mounted, D2 §2.3), the engine's own notes on the last save (parse errors, refused paths, §2.5) and the result schema; and dispatches the backend exactly as a one-shot run (D2 §1.1, §1.2: the same template, `--no-session-persistence`, a fresh volatile home). The role's reply is its structured result (`/surety/out/result.json`: `{reply, artifact_changed, questions_for_owner?, done?}`), accepted on exit class `clean` (D2 §1.4), appended to the transcript as the role's entry, and shown to the owner. **Provider-side continuation is not used:** no `--resume`, no restored provider files, no provider conversation state (D2 §1.7's rule applied to turns). Every turn therefore starts from durable records, which is what F §3.9.3 requires of a resumed session anyway, so resumption (§2.4) and an ordinary turn are the same construction. **Cost of this choice:** each turn re-sends the transcript; the provider's cache absorbs most of it when turns follow within its window, and the ledger records `cached_in` apart from `billable_in` (D1 §13.2) so the benchmark sees it (§7.3). **Alternative** (Q1 (b)): D2 §1.8's design, provider files carried as a `provider_files` record and restored, with the backend's own `--resume`; fewer tokens, but a dependency on provider file formats, a continuation qualification no canary has established (D2 §4.1 `session_qualified` false; AR N04), and a transcript the engine would hold only as the provider wrote it.

**2.3 The transcript is an engine record.** `session_transcripts`: one `transcript` record per session (D1 A.2 `RecordKind`), append-only by turn, each entry `{turn, speaker: owner | role | engine, at, text_ref}` with the text in the record through the redactor; the raw backend stream of each turn is the turn's own `transcript` record as for any run. Secrets the owner types are redacted by registered pattern only (D1 §14.2); §11 class C says what that cannot catch. Metering: each turn's ledger row names the provider, model requested and observed, billable and cached tokens and cost status (E16b; D1 §13); `GET /v1/projects/:p/runs/:r` shows a session's turns and spend live (E10: "the UI shows session spend live"; in M5 the CLI does).

**2.4 Save, close, idle, resume** (E10; D1 §15.2, §7.3, §7.4). **Save** (`surety session save`, `POST …/sessions/:r/save`) is admitted only at `open_idle`; it snapshots the workspace, validates the diff against the role's permitted paths (§2.5) and commits a `checkpoint` revision on the project's integration branch through the journal (D1 §7.4, §7.5), as an `intent` revision (D1 §7.8); `current_base` advances; a refused save leaves the workspace retained and tells the session why on the next turn. **Close** (`surety session close`) saves if the workspace differs from `current_base`, then enters `closing` and `endRun` (D1 §4.5); a role's `done: true` in its result proposes closing and the owner's command performs it. Closing raises the decision the role's product calls for (§3.4). **Idle timeout** (`session_idle_timeout`, default 20 m): the engine saves (as above; a refused save is recorded and the artifact stays in the retained workspace) and closes with outcome `timed_out`, reason `deadline` (D1 §4.1); nothing is lost that was in the workspace. **Budget exhaustion** between turns stops the session at that boundary (D1 §13.3; E7). **Resume** (`surety session resume <run>`) is D1 §15.3: a new run linked by `parent_run`, same role, a fresh grant, the workspace at the last committed checkpoint (a retained but uncommitted workspace is offered to the owner as `stash` or `discard` through the checkout integrity route, D1 §7.6, never silently adopted), and the transcript continued from the durable record; since every turn builds from durable records (§2.2), nothing is reconstructed that was not already there.

**2.5 What a session may write.** The role's permitted paths are its F §4.1 outputs and nothing else, enforced at save by D1 §7.3 step 1 (a diff outside them is `diff_violation`; the save is refused whole, the session is told, the workspace keeps the change for the owner to see): Vision `.surety/idea.md`; Spec Writer `.surety/spec/`; Architect `.surety/architecture/`, `.surety/adrs/`, `.surety/roadmap/`, `.surety/phases/`. None may touch source, `.surety/checks/` (protected, D1 §5.2) or `.surety/policy.json`. The engine, not the role, performs every commit (E2). A session's workspace is the project's repository at `current_base`, so the Architect reads the spec and the Spec Writer reads the idea without a copy.

**2.6 Qualification.** A session needs no provider continuation (§2.2), so no `session_headless` trust mode is activated (D2 A.2 stays reserved). What E10 asks to be qualified separately is established by one **continuation canary** added to D2 §7.2's attempt: two turns on the fixture project, the second instructed to answer from the first turn's transcript and artifact and seen to do so, with the first turn's domain terminated with closure before the second is dispatched (D2 §4.4's quiescence) and no provider file carried between them (inventoried, D2 §4.3). The entry records `session_qualified` true from that canary only; a one-shot entry without it refuses `session` runs with `backend_refused` (as built). Cost: one more paid canary per attempt, about the cost of the positive canary (M4: 0.05 USD for two).

**2.7 The client.** The CLI is a thin client (D1 §11.6): `surety session open <role> [--model <m>]`, `say <text>` or an interactive loop reading lines, `save`, `close`, `resume <run>`, `show <run>` (transcript and spend). Routes: `POST /v1/projects/:p/sessions {role, model?}`, `POST …/sessions/:r/turns {text}` (long-poll or SSE on `…/runs/:r/tail` for the reply), `…/save`, `…/close`, `…/resume`; the routes exist and are refused today (`src/api/server.ts`, `unsupported('Agent sessions')`), so M5 lifts a refusal rather than adding routes. One open session per project at a time (`max_concurrent_runs` counts it, §6.4); a session and a one-shot run may not share a project while the project has an open session, because both write the same branch and the integration lease serializes them anyway (D1 §7.5).

## 3. The three interactive roles and their products

Each role's prompt is engine-authored fixed text (D2 §1.2), the SAP's five phases (listen, reflect, explore, shape, document) recast per role, with the prohibitions of F §4.1 and the shape of its product. The prompts are M5's build artefacts; D6 fixes what each product is and how the engine reads it.

**3.1 Vision → the idea record** (F §4.1: "Conversation → idea record"; may not write spec, architecture, code or checks). Product: `.surety/idea.md`, IBT's sections filled, plus a closing **identity block** the engine parses: `name`, `one_line`, `users`, `non_goals`, `success_measures`, `sensitive_areas` (from F §5.6's closed list or `none`), `tier_recommendation` (T1, T2, T3 or `unsure`), `environments` (names). Everything else is prose for the Spec Writer and the owner. The engine reads the identity block at save and reports a block that does not parse. Approval: `idea_accept` (D1 A.8, not floor): the owner accepts, and the record's version is recorded on `projects.idea_version`; a project may be created with the idea and no session (§1 step 1).

**3.2 Spec Writer → the specification** (F §4.1: "Idea, change requests → versioned spec with acceptance criteria; proposed diffs"; may not approve its own spec, write architecture, code or checks). Product: `.surety/spec/spec.md` in TPL's exact shape: the header (`Spec version`, `Status: draft`, `Tier`, `Owner`), §4 one subsection per requirement `R<n>` with statement, rationale, sensitive areas and criteria `R<n>.<m>`, §5 the index table the engine parses (D3 §4.5; `src/checks/requirement-index.ts`), §6 modules and protected paths, §7 environments with D4's fields (target, runtime and start command, port variable, `artifact.exclude`, the required post-deploy behaviour check, stable arguments), §8 to §11. **What the engine reads and checks at every save:** the index parses (a row that does not refuses the save's acceptance as spec content and names the row, as D3 §4.5 refuses approval); every `R<n>` of §4 has a row and every row a subsection; each requirement has at least one criterion or is marked `human-test` in its subsection (a requirement with no criterion is uncertain and cannot be validated, D3 §4.3; the engine says so); sensitive areas are from the closed list; the tier is one of three; keys are never renumbered against the previous saved version (TPL: keys never change once confirmed; a key that disappears is `withdrawn`, not deleted). The findings are given to the session as the engine's entry on the next turn. The Spec Writer reads the idea record and the project's constraints; in a change request (§4) it reads the current approved spec and the request.

**3.3 Architect → architecture, ADRs, module map, roadmap, phase plan** (F §4.1: "Approved spec → ADRs, architecture, diagrams, module map, phase roadmap, detailed phase plans, proposed validation scope"; may not approve its own proposals, change an approved requirement silently, write code or acceptance checks). Products, each prose beside a machine file the engine parses (Appendix B gives the schemas):
- `.surety/architecture/architecture.md`: the architecture, its diagrams as text; `modules.json`: the module map, each `{name, paths, sensitive_areas, tier_override?, interfaces: [{name, kind, path?, description}]}`, refining TPL §6 (O6: the Architect defines modules and maps features to them). Every TPL §6 module must be kept or explicitly split or renamed with the mapping stated; a sensitive area on a TPL module can be moved but never dropped (F §5.6's floor is the spec's).
- `.surety/adrs/ADR-<nnn>.md`: one decision each, with `key`, `status`, `context`, `decision`, `consequences`; the texts the context packages carry (as built, `baseline_texts` kind `adr`).
- `.surety/roadmap/roadmap.json`: `{version, architecture_version, phases: [{number, goal, modules, requirement_ids, depends_on}]}` with `roadmap.md` beside it (F §3.10.1). Every approved requirement appears in exactly one phase (F §3.10.2: "every approved requirement is traceable to its planned phase"); the check refuses a roadmap that leaves one out or lists one twice.
- `.surety/phases/phase-<n>.json`: `{phase, prepared_against: {spec_version, architecture_version, roadmap_version, revision}, stages: [{number, goal, tasks: [text], modules, implements, adrs, depends_on: [stage numbers], validation: {criteria: [R<n>.<m>], kinds: [CheckKind], sensitive_areas}, integration_obligations: [text]}], conformance: within_baseline | uncertain, notes}` with `phase-<n>.md` beside it (F §3.10.1: build stages with tasks, validation scope and integration obligations; default 3 to 5 stages, `phase_size`). The validation block is the "proposed validation scope" of F §4.1 and the input of F §3.10.6's fourth check; it proposes, and the Verifier's checks and D3 §4.2's required set decide.

**3.4 How each product is approved** (F §3.2, §3.3, §9.1, §9.2; E9's inventory). Closing a session raises the decision its role's product calls for, with the parsed machine files as `observed` evidence and the prose as `claimed` (D1 §3.4 `Provenance`), and the session's run ends; the decision is answered later, from the queue, like any other (D1 §10). **Vision:** `idea_accept`, not floor. **Spec Writer:** `spec_approval`, **floor** (F §9.1); the option `approve` is blocked (D1 §9.7's blockers on the option) while the index does not parse, a requirement has no criterion and no `human-test` mark, or an open question of TPL §10 blocks a requirement the roadmap hint assigns to phase 1 (§12.2 reading 1); its effect: `spec_revisions` row, `registerRequirementIndex` (as built), `ensureModules` from TPL §6, environments by name (`configureEnvironment` with no adapter, as built, "nothing is ever deployed to it" until D4's configuration), `baseline_state` `spec_ready`, the change log row written by the engine. **Architect:** `architecture_approval` for the architecture, module map and ADRs, consolidated (D1 §10.3, one `batch_key`) with `plan_approval` for phase 1, raised only after the mechanical check passed (a failing check returns to the session as the engine's entry, and close is refused until it passes or the owner closes with `--refuse`, which records the products and raises nothing). Both are human in M5; the automated prototype policy of F §9.2 is not built (§11 class B). Effect: `architecture_revisions`, `modules` replaced by the map, `roadmap_revisions` 1, `phase_plans` 1 with `registerPlan` (K4), stages and their work. A later Architect **session** opened by the owner on an existing architecture (to revise an ADR or the roadmap) raises `architecture_approval` again on close with the diff shown; the engine's impact (§4.4's function over ADRs and roadmap) is its evidence.

**3.5 Context packages** (F §3.10.8; D2 §1.3). Vision: the idea record and IBT. Spec Writer: the accepted idea record, TPL, the sensitive-area list, the tier table (F §5.7 in the engine's words), and for a change request the approved spec and the request. Architect (session): the approved spec with its index, TPL §6 and §7, the foundations' planning rules in the engine's words (phase size, one phase at a time, every requirement assigned, validation per criterion), Appendix B's schemas, and the repository as it is. Architect (replan, §5.6): the current spec and index, every ADR, the current roadmap and module map, the completed phase's plan with each stage's `integrated_revision`, the phase gate's evaluation and the per-criterion check states, every finding of the phase with its disposition, the deferrals outstanding, the open objections, and the repository at the integration head; never the first session's transcript (F §3.10.5 step 2).

## 4. The specification as a governed artifact

**4.1 The baseline.** `spec_revisions` (D1 A.3): `version`, `git_path` `.surety/spec/spec.md`, `git_blob`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `supersedes`. Approval is the only writer; the blob it names is the one committed at the session's last save, and the approval's effect plan (D1 §9.7) names that blob, so a save between preview and answer stales the decision. `requirements` rows carry `spec_revision`, `key`, `text` (as built), `criteria`, `sensitive_areas`, `assigned_phase` (from the roadmap, §5.1), `status` (`pending` until a gate evaluation verifies its criteria, D1 §3.1; `withdrawn` is added to `RequirementStatus`, A.2). A requirement's key never changes (TPL §4); a withdrawn one keeps its row.

**4.2 The change request** (F §3.8 step 1). `POST /v1/projects/:p/spec/change-requests {text | file}` (`surety spec change-request`; the route D1 §11.4 names) by the owner, or by the engine from a `requirement_conflict` finding's `change_spec` answer (D3 §5 X2). It records a `spec_change_requests` row `{seq, spec_revision (current), text →records, source: owner | finding →findings, status}` and creates `spec_change` work (D1 A.2; K8 gives it a run). One request at a time per project is dispatched; others wait `eligible` so impacts are computed against one current version.

**4.3 The proposed diff** (F §3.8 step 2). The `spec_change` work's run is a Spec Writer one-shot (or, on the owner's `--session`, a session) whose workspace is the repository at the integration head; it may write `.surety/spec/` only; its result names the changed requirements and the reason per change. The snapshot is validated (D1 §7.3) and committed, **not to the integration branch**, but as an `intent` revision on a registered side ref `refs/surety/spec-change/<seq>` (D1 §7.2 `RefKind` gains `spec_change`, A.2), journaled like any ref write; the proposal binds that revision's blob. Nothing of the current spec changes yet.

**4.4 Impact analysis** (F §3.8 step 3), computed by the engine from durable records and the two blobs, recorded as a `spec_impact` record (provenance `observed`) and as the decision's evidence:
- **Requirements:** per key, `added`, `withdrawn`, `text_changed`, `criteria_added`, `criteria_removed`, `criteria_changed` (a criterion's text differs), `areas_changed`; a key renumbered is a refusal of the diff (`spec_keys_renumbered`), not an impact.
- **Checks** (D3 §1.4, §3.4): every check of the effective protected version whose `covers.criteria` names a removed or changed criterion (it will be a discovery error `criterion_unknown` or an uncovered criterion after approval), and every added criterion with no check (an uncovered criterion, `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` to come).
- **ADRs and architecture:** ADRs whose text cites an affected key (a textual reference `R<n>` in the ADR's body); modules whose sensitive areas the change alters.
- **Stages and roadmap:** stages whose `implements` includes an affected requirement, with their status; the phases of the roadmap holding affected or added requirements; whether the change touches the current phase (early replanning, F §3.10.7) or only later ones.
- **Evidence:** every `check_results` row whose check covers an affected criterion, and every gate evaluation that selected one, listed as to be invalidated; findings naming an affected criterion.
- **Work:** open or running work items touching affected stages.
Nothing is estimated: an impact the engine cannot compute (an unreadable blob, an unparsable index) refuses the proposal with the reason, never an empty impact.

**4.5 Approval and invalidation** (F §3.8 steps 4 and 5; floor, F §9.1 "human approval of the project spec"). `spec_change` is raised with the impact and the diff; its options are `approve` and `reject`; its manifest binds the current spec version, the side ref's revision, the effective protected version and the impact's hash, so any change stales it (D1 §4.6). On `approve`, one effect: the side revision is integrated onto the integration branch through the journal (D1 §7.5, a rebase if the head moved, the spec files alone changing); `spec_revisions` version n+1 with `supersedes`; requirements re-registered (keys kept; withdrawn marked; `assigned_phase` kept where the roadmap still names the key, null for an added one); the change log row appended by the engine; `baseline.spec_approved` with `version`. Then, in the same finalizer: every listed result invalidated (`invalidated_at`, reason `spec_changed`), every listed evaluation stale, the effective protected version's discovery errors recomputed (D3 §3.4) so gates carry `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` until corrected, `check_correction` work registered for the Verifier naming the affected and uncovered criteria (D3 §2.11's route), and `replan` work registered when the impact touches the current or a later phase (F §3.10.7, with the impact as its triggering evidence); an added requirement with no phase blocks the `phase` gate of every later phase (`REQUIREMENT_UNASSIGNED`) until a roadmap revision places it. On `reject`, the side ref is kept as history (`keep`), the request `rejected`. **Evidence reuse across a spec change is not automatic** (F §3.8): D6 creates no applicability assessment; the runner re-runs (D3 §3.5), which costs no model.

**4.6 How a change flows to stages, checks and gates.** A stage whose requirement was withdrawn keeps its history; if it is `planned`, the replan removes or retargets it; if `building`, its run completes and the replan decides; if `integrated` or `verified`, nothing is undone (source changes are new candidates, F §3.8's table). A changed criterion makes its check `criterion_unknown` at discovery, so every gate is `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` naming the check until the Verifier's correction lands as a protected proposal (tightening or otherwise, D3 §3); the stage gate of an already-complete stage is not re-evaluated for completion (the work is terminal), but every later gate, `phase` and `alpha_authorize` included, sees the new version (D1 §9.1: scope from the candidate and the approved bindings as they are now). A candidate nominated before the change keeps `spec_revision` as a historical fact (D1 A.3); its evaluations under the new version are new evaluations.

**4.7 What is not a spec change.** Moving a requirement between phases is a roadmap revision (F §3.10.2), by the Architect's replan or a session, recorded `baseline.roadmap_revised` with the move visible; it changes `assigned_phase` and nothing else. A module split or an ADR change is an architecture revision (§3.4). Neither touches `spec_revisions`.

## 5. Planning

**5.1 The roadmap** (F §3.10.1, §3.10.2). `roadmap_revisions` (D1 A.3): `version`, `architecture_revision`, `git_path`, `phases` from `roadmap.json`. Written by the Architect's session (version 1) or a replan (a revision); every version is recorded, none edited. The engine derives `requirements.assigned_phase` from the current version, and the project read shows each requirement with its phase and its delivery status per candidate (D1 §9.1, computed; F §3.10.2 "visibly pending"). Phase dependencies must be acyclic and each phase's modules a subset of the module map.

**5.2 The phase plan** (F §3.10.1). `phase_plans`: `roadmap_revision`, `phase_number`, `prepared_against_spec`, `prepared_against_architecture` (added, A.3), `prepared_against_revision`, `git_path`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `completed_at` (added), `verification →gate_evaluations?` (added). Exactly one plan is open per project: the next phase's, produced only after the previous phase is complete (F §3.10.1 "produced only for the next phase"); an attempt to register a plan for a later phase is refused (`phase_not_next`). Stages (`stages`, as built, plus K4's `depends_on`, `tasks`, `validation`, `integration_obligations`) number 3 to 5 by default, `phase_size` `{min, max}` per project (F §9.2); a plan outside the bound is `uncertain` and goes to the human, never refused outright, because a two-stage phase at the end of a project is sense, not error.

**5.3 The mechanical check** (F §3.10.6; `checkPlan(plan, roadmap?)`, a pure function of the plan's machine files and the store, recorded as a `plan_check` record and on the plan row). Each clause yields a reason code (A.4) with its subjects; a plan with any reason is `refused` (`baseline.plan_refused`) and returned to its author:
1. **References.** Every `implements` key is a requirement of the current spec revision and not withdrawn (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_REQUIREMENT`); every module is in the current module map (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_MODULE`); every ADR key exists under `.surety/adrs/` at the plan's revision (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_ADR`); every `validation.criteria` key is a criterion of a requirement the stage implements (`PLAN_CRITERION_MISMATCH`); every `depends_on` names a stage of this plan (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_STAGE`).
2. **Approvals.** `prepared_against` equals the current approved spec, architecture and roadmap versions and the integration head (`PLAN_STALE_BASELINE`); a replan prepared against a head that moved by an engine commit since is re-based mechanically (the plan's files are intent artifacts) and its `prepared_against_revision` updated; one prepared against an older spec version is refused.
3. **Dependencies.** The stage graph is acyclic (`PLAN_CYCLE`); the phase's stages touch only modules the roadmap lists for the phase (`PLAN_MODULE_OUTSIDE_PHASE`); every requirement the roadmap assigns to the phase is implemented by some stage of the plan, or the plan carries a roadmap revision moving it (`PLAN_REQUIREMENT_UNPLANNED`); every phase the roadmap says this one depends on is complete (`PLAN_DEPENDENCY_INCOMPLETE`).
4. **Validation obligations.** Every criterion of every requirement assigned to the phase is in some stage's `validation.criteria` (`PLAN_CRITERION_UNVALIDATED`); every sensitive area of those requirements and of the phase's modules is in some stage's `validation.sensitive_areas` (`PLAN_AREA_UNVALIDATED`); the kinds F §5.7 requires at the scope's tier appear (`PLAN_KIND_MISSING`), using D3 §4.3's coverage function over the planned set in place of the required set, as D3 §4.3 anticipated.

**5.4 Routing** (F §3.10.6 "approval routing"). A plan that passes and declares `conformance: within_baseline`, changes no phase goal of the current roadmap, carries no ADR change and no roadmap revision, and needs no spec change, is **registered without a human** (`baseline.plan_approved`, `approved_by` `engine:plan_check`). A plan that changes a phase goal, adds or alters an ADR, carries a roadmap revision that moves a requirement, needs a spec change (its notes say so, or it implements a key the spec lacks), or declares `uncertain`, raises `plan_approval` (D1 A.8; not floor) with the check's record and the diff of the roadmap and ADRs as evidence; the human's `approve` registers it, `reject` returns it to the Architect as a new `replan` with the note. A `requirement_conflict` the Architect reports in its result (a requirement it cannot plan) is a finding routed to `change_spec` (D3 X2), never a silent change (F §4.1).

**5.5 Registration** (D1 §7.8; `registerPlan`, K4). The integration finalizer of the Architect's run (session save at close, or the replan's one-shot commit) registers, atomically with the commit's confirmation: the `phase_plans` row; the `stages` rows with `depends_on` resolved to stage ids and `status` `planned`; one `stage_build` work item per stage from trigger `(plan, <stage>, 1)`, with `depends_on` the work items of the stages it depends on **and** the completion of the phase's `check_authoring` work (§6.2); the `check_authoring` work itself, trigger `(phase_plan, <plan>, 1)`. A committed plan is schedulable by construction and nothing else can create stage work (CH incident 11).

**5.6 Re-entry after each phase** (F §3.10.5). When the `phase` gate is satisfied and `phase_verification` work complete (§5.8), the finalizer records `completed_at` and creates `replan` work, trigger `(phase_complete, <plan>, 1)`, if the roadmap has a next phase. Its run is an Architect **one-shot** (decision Q2, recommended (a)): deadline `deadline_architect` (30 m), the context package of §3.5, permitted paths as §2.5, result `{plan: phase-<n+1>, roadmap_revision?: ..., adrs_changed?: [...], conformance, notes}`. Its commit is an `intent` revision on the integration branch whose finalizer runs §5.3 and §5.4. A refused plan ends the run `failed` with `invalid_result` naming the reasons; the `replan` item takes one automatic repair with the reasons in its package (D1 §4.3's repair accounting, `repair_attempts_max`), then parks with a blocker for the owner, who may open an Architect session on it instead. When the roadmap has no next phase, the project's build is complete and §6.8 applies.

**5.7 Early replanning** (F §3.10.7). `replan` work is also created, with the triggering evidence on its trigger, by: an approved spec change touching the current or a later phase (§4.5); a blocker on a stage's work parked at `repair_attempts_max` or `no_progress_max` whose decision the owner answers `replan` (a new option on `blocker` for stage work, A.2); an objection assessed and upheld as being against the plan (the `blocker` of D3 X2 answered `replan`); a `contract_conflict` finding the Reviewer raises against the plan with disposition `fix` scoped to the plan. Filing alone triggers nothing (F §3.10.7). The early replan's plan **replaces the open phase plan's unbuilt remainder**: stages `planned` are cancelled with their work (`work.cancelled`), stages `building` finish their run and are then re-evaluated against the new plan (kept if unchanged by number, goal and implements; else their work is cancelled after integration and the code stays as working revisions), stages `integrated` or `verified` are kept; the new plan's `prepared_against_revision` is the integration head. Every early replan records which tasks, assumptions and checks became stale, from the impact of §4.4 or the blocker's evidence (F §3.10.7).

**5.8 Phase verification and the `phase` gate** (F §3.10.4, §5.6 cadence; D3 §4.2, §4.3, which defined the scope and left the gate `unsupported`). When every stage of the open plan is `verified` (its `stage` gate satisfied on a candidate holding it by ancestry), the engine: (1) if the latest integrated revision is not a nominated candidate (T1 projects, E11 "at phase completion"), nominates it (`engine_cadence`); (2) evaluates `phase` for that candidate and the plan: scope every requirement assigned to the phase, delivered or not (D3 §4.2 (b)), the regression set being every required check of every stage of this and earlier phases at the scope's tier, plus the floor; (3) registers `phase_verification` work for the Verifier: a run that reads the evaluation and the phase's findings, may raise findings and propose corrections, and signs off the phase at T2 and T3 (`signoffs.scope` gains `phase`, A.2); (4) when the gate is satisfied and the sign-off recorded, marks the plan `completed_at` and every stage of it `verified`, emits `baseline.phase_complete`, and creates the `replan` (§5.6). A `phase` gate not satisfied names its reasons like any gate; a requirement assigned to the phase with no delivering stage is `PLAN_REQUIREMENT_UNPLANNED` on the gate too. **Phase completion establishes nothing about deployment:** `alpha_authorize` keeps its own scope over the whole delivered set (F §3.10.4).

## 6. The build's execution

**6.1 What M3 and M4 built and M5 keeps unchanged.** The Builder run in a `role` domain with a scoped context package (D2); snapshot, validation, integration through the journal (D1 §7.3, §7.5); nomination by cadence (E11; D3 L3); check registration and the runner (D3 §2); the Verifier and Reviewer runs, findings, dispositions, repairs and the fix loop (E43; D3 §2.10, §2.11); the protected update path and the classifier (D3 §3); the stage gate and `alpha_authorize` (D1 §9); the deployment, verification and `alpha_complete` (D4). M5 adds what makes them run **in sequence over many stages and phases without a hand on the wheel**: dependencies and ordering (§6.4), the autonomy profile (§6.5), check authoring per phase (§6.2), the phase gate and replan (§5), the deploy request policy (§6.8), and the pace instruments (§7.6).

**6.2 Check authoring per phase** (F §3.10.3, §5.1; decision settled, §12.2 reading 4). `check_authoring` work (A.2, new kind; transitions as `check_correction`) is registered with each phase plan (§5.5) and runs before any stage of the phase: a Verifier run whose package is `src/checks/guide.ts`'s (the governed values, the index, the stages of the plan with their `implements` and `validation`, the environments, D3 Appendix B) plus the module map's `interfaces` (so the Verifier knows the paths and entry points stages will create, which is what E103's and E135's first-try defects lacked) and the architecture's text. It writes definitions and programs for every criterion the plan's stages validate, the tier's kinds, the floor's areas, and (first phase) the post-deploy checks of TPL §7. The diff is a protected proposal (D1 §7.3 step 2), classified by D3 §3.1 (`tightening` when it only adds checks with valid definitions under declared inputs; `unclassifiable` if it touches `check_commands`, which the first phase must to name the toolchain, so **the first phase's proposal goes to the human under every authority**, and D6 says so plainly: one human act per project for the toolchain), applied by D1 §7.9. Checks for interfaces that do not yet exist are written against the plan's interfaces and fail until built; a check the Verifier cannot write yet is listed in its result as `deferred_criteria` with the reason, and the engine registers a second `check_authoring` for them after the stage that creates the interface is integrated (F §5.1: "additional checks may be introduced when interfaces become executable"). Stage work depends on the phase's first `check_authoring` completing (`depends_on`, §5.5), so no Builder builds before the checks exist (F §5.1's default); the exception F §5.1 allows needs a recorded reason, which is the owner's `blocker` answer `build_first` on the authoring item if it parks.

**6.3 A stage's chain** (as built, with K3). `stage_build` eligible → Builder run (`deadline_builder`) → snapshot, validation, integration (CAS; a rebase if another stage integrated meanwhile, D1 §7.5) → `stages.status` `integrated`, `integrated_revision` → nomination at T2 and T3 (`engine_cadence`), at T1 on the Builder's request or at phase completion → checks registered (trigger `nomination`) and run by the runner, serially per candidate (`max_concurrent_checks`) → `verification` work → Verifier run (reads the results and the code read-only, raises findings, proposes corrections) → at T2 and T3 `review` work once every required check passed (`queueReview`, as built) → Reviewer run (sign-off bound to the acceptance content hash, or findings) → `stage` gate evaluated at each write (D1 §9.5) → satisfied: `completeStageWork`, `stages.status` `verified`, the dependants' `depends_on` met. Repairs: a failed required check sends the item `verifying → eligible` once per candidate (D3 §2.10); a `fix` disposition creates fix work (E43) whose integration nominates the fix candidate at every tier (E104); `repair_attempts_max` (3) and `no_progress_max` (2) park with a `blocker`. A Builder's objection stops repair and asks the owner (`correct_check`, `change_spec`, `retry`, `cancel`; D3 X2), and §5.7 adds `replan`.

**6.4 Ordering, dependencies and what runs in parallel** (decision Q4, recommended (a); K2, K4). **Order** is the plan's: a stage's work is eligible when every `depends_on` stage is `integrated` (its code is on the branch), not `verified`; the default the Architect is told to use is a chain (stage n depends on n−1), and it declares independence explicitly. **Pipelining:** with stage n integrated, stage n+1's Builder may run while stage n's checks, Verifier and Reviewer run; a stage n finding dispositioned `fix` makes fix work that integrates after n+1's build (the rebase path), and the fix candidate supersedes; stage n+1's stage gate is evaluated on a candidate that holds both by ancestry (as built). **Parallel builds:** two stages with no dependency between them may build at once in two workspaces; their integrations serialize on the integration lease, the second rebased and re-validated (D1 §7.5). **What permits it:** project `max_concurrent_runs` raised from 1 (BS §6 correction 20) to a bound, default 2 (K2), under three rules kept from D1: one run per work item; integration serial; a session counts as a run and admits no one-shot beside it (§2.7). **What bounds it:** D2 §3.7's envelope: each role domain reserves `domain_memory_max` (8 GiB default) and the host keeps `host_reserve_memory`; two Builders at the default need about 18 GiB, more than the workstation showed free during M4's runs (E130 item 1: never below 12.4 GiB), so concurrency 2 is real only with a lower per-domain memory for roles, a project setting (`domain_memory_max` is engine-scoped today; K2 makes a project override possible, never above the engine's). The numeric is Sean's (Q4). **Verifier and Reviewer are never parallel on one candidate** (the review waits for the verification, as built), and a Reviewer reviewing a candidate never shares a project with a Builder integrating onto it before the review's content hash is bound (D1 §3.4 binds the hash at claim, as built, so a later integration changes nothing the Reviewer signed).

**6.5 Autonomy within a phase** (F §9.2 "autonomy may schedule and execute eligible work within role contracts"; decision Q3, recommended (a); K3). Today's `max_chained_roles` (default 1) stops after every role for a human `continue` (D1-34; `chainBoundary`). D6 replaces the counter's meaning with an **autonomy profile** per project, `autonomy: step | stage | phase`, applied by the same boundary mechanism: `step`, as today, a decision after every role run; `stage`, the chain Builder → checks → Verifier → Reviewer → repairs runs unattended and the boundary falls before the next stage's Builder; `phase`, every stage of the open plan, the phase verification and the replan run unattended, and the boundary falls only where F routes a decision: a `plan_approval` (§5.4), a spec change, a loosening or unclassifiable correction, a Medium or higher disposition the Reviewer cannot make (O3), a blocker, a parked item. `max_chained_roles` stays as a hard upper bound on runs per chain within a profile (default raised to 24 under `phase`), so a loop can never run away: the fix loop is already bounded by `repair_attempts_max` and `no_progress_max`, and every run by its deadline and budget (D1 §13.3). **Nothing on F §9.1's floor moves:** the spec and its changes, protected changes through the update process, deployment verification, the Builder's inability to accept its own work. The benchmark (§7.7) runs at `phase`; the recommended default for a new project is `phase` too, because `step` is what made Verity's human the step that never ran (CH R13; AH §5.5).

**6.6 Repair, findings and objections across stages** (as built; D3 §2.10, §2.11, X2; E43). Unchanged in rule; two additions. **A finding against an earlier stage** found by a later stage's Verifier is scoped `lineage` and applies to every successor (D1 §9.3 input 5, as built), so the fix is one `fix` item whichever stage is current. **A stage parked** (`repair_attempts_max`, `no_progress_max`, or an upheld objection against the plan) does not stop independent stages: its dependants wait (`depends_on` unmet) while the owner's `blocker` offers `retry`, `replan` (§5.7), `cancel`; the phase cannot complete while any of its stages is not `verified`.

**6.7 The phase boundary.** §5.8 then §5.6. Under `autonomy: phase` the replan is registered and dispatched with no human unless routed; under `stage` or `step` the boundary falls before the replan's dispatch. The owner can at any time open an Architect session on the project (§3.4), which takes the integration lease's place for `.surety/` writes while open.

**6.8 Alpha** (D4 §1; F §3.3, §3.10.4). When the roadmap's last phase is complete, or at any phase's completion under policy, the deploy request of D4 §4.1 is made: by the owner's command (`surety deploy c-N --env alpha`, D4 Q3, the default), or by the engine under the project policy `alpha_deploy: on_phase_complete` (decision Q5, recommended (a)): the engine raises the `deploy` trigger itself (`observeTrigger` already admits an engine-raised `deploy`, `work.ts`) for the newest candidate whose `phase` gate is satisfied, after the environment is configured (D4 §3.2) and the adapter qualified (D4 §2.6), and never otherwise. Everything after the request is D4's, unchanged: `alpha_authorize` over the delivered requirement set with the floor and the post-deploy obligations (J4), the seal, the deploy, the two identity reads and the behaviour checks, `alpha_complete`, `candidate.advanced`. No approval is on F §9.1's floor for Alpha, so the automatic request lowers nothing; what it removes is one command. A mid-project Alpha is accepted against the requirements delivered so far, the rest shown pending (F §3.10.4).

## 7. Pace

**7.1 What this section claims.** Where wall-clock time and tokens go in a Surety-run build, by construction and from the few measurements that exist; what F P3 and §9.2 scale with risk; what keeps a stage near Verity's pace; and the instruments M5 records so that E130 item 9's benchmark can be run. **No figure here is a prediction the acceptance plan pins**; the rows of Appendix C pin that the instruments exist and are honest.

**7.2 Where a stage's time goes** (T2, the common case), in the order of §6.3, with the mechanism and the lever:

| Step | Mechanism | Time | Lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eligible → dispatched | the tick (D1 §1.5, `tick_interval` 30 s); the envelope (D2 §3.7) | 0 to 30 s per step today; a chain of eight engine steps can idle up to 4 min | **K1:** a transition that makes work eligible, ends a run, records a result or finalizes an operation requests a tick (D1 §1.5's flag, set internally), so idle latency is the tick's duration, not its interval |
| Sandbox build and launch | the launcher, the mount plan's validation, the cgroup, the domain init (D2 §2.2, §3.2) | seconds; not yet measured apart from a run | measured by §7.6 |
| Builder's model time | the backend, the scoped package (F §3.10.8) | **the dominant term**; M3 and M4 real runs: 20 to 70 s and 5 to 7 k billable input tokens for a one-file stage; a real stage is minutes to tens of minutes (Verity: fixture C 857 s whole) | the package's scope; the model per role (F §9.2); the stage's size (3 to 5 stages per phase, F §3.10.1) |
| Snapshot, validation, integration | engine git (D1 §7.3, §7.5) | seconds; a rebase adds a validation | none needed |
| Checks | the runner, no model, serial per candidate (`max_concurrent_checks` 1) | the toolchain's time per check, seconds to a minute each; M4's identity reads about 100 ms | `max_concurrent_checks` per project (D3 §2.5), bounded by the envelope |
| Verifier run | model | M3 and M4: 25 to 70 s, 9 to 18 k billable in, 0.06 to 0.16 USD on trivial stages | the package carries the results and the diff, not the repository's history |
| Reviewer run (T2, T3) | model | M3: about 30 s, 9 k in, 0.06 USD on a trivial stage | T1 has none (F §5.7) |
| Gate evaluation | the engine | sub-second | none |
| Repairs | one Builder run per failed check round, bounded | the Builder's time again; M4's real run: one build, one repair in try 1 | the Verifier's package (§6.2's interfaces), so checks fail for the right reason |
| Human | none within a phase under `phase` autonomy (§6.5) | 0, or the owner's time-to-decision where routed | the profile; the queue's targets (E9) |

A T2 stage on a real project is therefore the sum of three model runs plus repairs, with the engine adding seconds and, after K1, no idle. Verity's per-stage figure is not recorded separately in the predecessor reviews (CH records whole fixtures and calendar days), so the comparison the benchmark makes is per fixture (§7.7).

**7.3 Where tokens go.** Per stage: the Builder's package (requirements, ADRs, constraints, the phase plan's stage, the interfaces of dependency modules; F §3.10.8) plus what it reads; the Verifier's (results, outputs, the diff); the Reviewer's (the diff and the hash). Per phase, once: the Verifier's check authoring and the Architect's replan. Per project, once: the three sessions, whose tokens are the owner's conversation, re-sent each turn (§2.2) with `cached_in` recorded apart. **What Surety does not spend:** planning per tick (Verity's planner thrash, 54 % of one run's tokens, about 4 M in another: AH §5.5, CH §3.5) is structurally absent, because a plan is registered once by a finalizer and replanning has four named triggers (F §3.10.7); re-bought reviews (CH incident 10) are absent, because no approval launches a run (D2 §4.1, D1 §10.5); unknown cost is never zero (C4). The ledger separates billable, cached and output tokens per run and per role (D1 §13; E16b), which is the benchmark's unit.

**7.4 Scaled to risk** (F P3, §5.7, §9.2). T1: no Reviewer; verification once per candidate, at phase completion or on the Builder's request (E11; F §5.6), so a phase's stages build back to back with the checks running after each integration and one Verifier run at the end; the kinds `acceptance` and `smoke` only. T2: per-stage nomination, Verifier and Reviewer; `integration` and `security_lint`. T3: per-module sign-offs, `property` and `failure_recovery`, a security review. The floor never scales down (F §5.6, §9.1). A T1 project at `phase` autonomy is the fastest Surety can run, and it is the Switchboard pace F's §1 asks for; a T3 project is slower by construction and should be.

**7.5 What keeps a Surety-run stage near Verity's pace.** (1) No human within a phase (§6.5), where Verity's stages waited for `verity review` and merge by hand or by a worker whose gates a human opened (CH §5.2, R13). (2) Event-driven ticks (K1). (3) Pipelining and bounded parallel builds (§6.4). (4) Checks without a model, serial per candidate but concurrent with runs. (5) Planning once per phase. (6) Fixed role prompts and a package stable within a phase, so the provider's cache serves repeated turns and runs. (7) The Verifier's package carrying the plan's interfaces, so fewer repair rounds are spent on checks that could never pass (E103, E135 try 1). **What makes it slower than Verity, and is kept:** the independent Verifier and Reviewer runs per stage at T2 (Verity ran a reviewer per stage too), the checks running before the Builder sees its result (Verity's CI ran after merge), and the engine's refusal to count anything it did not observe.

**7.6 The measurements M5 records** (D1 §12.2: projections from entities, never a second state). Every figure below is derived from rows that exist or that K1 to K9 add, and read through `GET /v1/projects/:p/pace` (`surety pace [--json]`), with `unknown` where a row is missing:
- **Per run:** `created_at`, `claimed_at`, `launched_at` (the `launched` status observation), `finished_at`; wait (created to launched), model time (launched to the terminal observation), end protocol (terminal to `ended`); billable, cached and output tokens, cost and its status, model observed; outcome and reason class; repair ordinal.
- **Per stage:** eligible (dependencies met) to `integrated`; `integrated` to the first `stage` evaluation; to `verified`; the counts of Builder, Verifier, Reviewer and fix runs; checks run and their total duration; repairs; findings by severity; the human wait (sum of open time of decisions in the stage's `blocked_while_open`).
- **Per phase:** plan registered to `completed_at`; check authoring's time and proposal class; stages; phase verification; replan time and its routing (mechanical or human).
- **Per project:** `project.created` to `baseline.spec_approved`; to `baseline.architecture_approved`; to each `baseline.phase_complete`; to `candidate.advanced` `alpha_deployed`; sessions' turns, time and tokens; totals of everything above; idle time (ticks on which eligible work was not dispatched, by hold: `resource_envelope`, chain boundary, `depends_on`, open decision).
- **The host:** engine version, host qualification, backend entry (binary hash, model), `max_concurrent_runs`, `autonomy`, `tick_interval`, `domain_memory_max`, so two runs are comparable (AH §7.2: "comparisons need the fixture commit/hash").
The read is a projection; it never changes an `observed_at` and never calls a backend (D1 §11.3).

**7.7 The benchmark after M5** (E130 item 9; not part of M5's acceptance). ## 8. The retrofit path, as far as E40 allows

E6 (F §3.12) adopts an existing repository: the engine pins its revision as the first candidate; the owner chooses `full` or `scoped` baseline mode; the Spec Writer and Architect run **in retrofit mode**, code as input, producing a descriptive spec, module map, sensitivity classification and ADRs with claim-versus-reality tables, every descriptive requirement marked reverse-engineered until a human confirms it (`requirement_confirm`); a conformance phase carries the code unchanged through the protected path to Alpha. **What D6 settles:** the sessions of §2 and §3 are the mechanism (E6: "no new roles"); retrofit mode is a context-package variant (the repository is the input, the prompt asks for description, the identity block and index carry `reverse_engineered: true` per requirement) and a `RequirementStatus` `descriptive_unverified` the engine already has (D1 A.2); the conformance phase is a one-stage phase plan whose stage implements nothing and whose validation covers the confirmed requirements, through §5 and §6 unchanged; `POST /v1/projects/adopt` and `adoption_mode` exist (D1 §11.4, A.8). **What M5 does not build** (E40: scope frozen to the journey; E136: "idea to Alpha"): retrofit mode's prompts and the claim-versus-reality table; the scoped baseline's per-phase descriptive criteria; `requirement_confirm`'s flow; the conformance phase's registration; adoption of an already-public repository (the delivery repository is D5's). Dogfooding Surety on itself (D1 §19.3) waits for that.

## 9. Boundary and security notes

Sessions change nothing of D2's boundary: a turn is a one-shot invocation in a `role` domain with the validated mount plan, the proxy as its only egress and no control-plane reach (D2 §2). What is new is the owner's typed text entering a prompt: it is the owner's own and trusted (D2 §2.1), redacted by registered patterns before it is recorded (D1 §14.2), and never a `raw_user_report` (E5). The Architect's and Spec Writer's products are committed content and therefore untrusted input to every later role (D2 §2.1: "the repository's committed content, which roles write"); the engine parses their machine files with closed schemas (Appendix B) and refuses unknown fields, as it parses check definitions (D3 §1.4). A plan is never executed; it is registered. Automatic deployment under `alpha_deploy` requests nothing a human could not request and passes the same gate (D4 J3, J4); the capability stays the Release Operator's (D4 §8). The autonomy profile moves no floor item (§6.5). An engine-requested tick (K1) runs the same tick (D1 §1.5), one at a time.

## 11. What D6 does not claim

In the form of `docs/acceptance/reports/M4-not-claimed.md`: class A is outside the threat model, class B is real behaviour no test has yet established or that M5 does not build, class C is a limitation accepted by design.

**Class A.** As D2 §8 class A. An owner who approves a specification he did not read, or an architecture he did not understand: the floor is his approval, and the engine shows him the parsed index and the check's record, not a judgement of their sense. A Spec Writer or Architect that writes what the owner asks for when the owner asks for something unwise.

**Class B, not built in M5.** Retrofit mode and everything §8 lists. Sessions on Codex, or on Claude Code by API key (the real lane is the subscription template; the API-key template is qualified one-shot only until its canary runs). Provider-native session continuation (Q1 (b)). The automated architecture approval of F §9.2. Evidence reuse across a spec change (F §3.8's applicability assessment; D6 re-runs instead). A UI for sessions and the queue (E14). Beta, Live, rollback and reattachment (D5). More than one open session per project. Parallel Builders at the default memory limit on the workstation (§6.4's envelope arithmetic). **Class B, not yet established:** that a Spec Writer session ends in a specification an owner approves, that an Architect's plan passes the mechanical check first time, that a real stage's Builder, Verifier and Reviewer take the times §7.2 extrapolates, and that pipelining shortens a phase: each is a measurement §7.6 makes and the benchmark reports, not a property a test can pin.

**Class C.** **Judgement:** the mechanical plan check proves references, approvals, dependencies and coverage, never that the plan is sensible, the ADRs sound or the stages well cut; the owner's architecture approval and the Reviewer's findings are the controls, as D3 §6 says of checks. A planned criterion with a vacuous check still passes D3's classifier (D3 §6 class C). **The specification:** the engine reads TPL §5's index and the headers; prose it does not read can contradict the index, and the Spec Writer is asked to keep them consistent, not made to. A requirement marked `human-test` has no check and is never `verified` by a gate; it stays visibly pending and the owner knows it. **Sessions:** the transcript is re-sent each turn, so a long session costs more per turn as it goes, and the cache's effect is the provider's to give; the redactor catches registered forms only, so a secret the owner types in an unregistered form reaches the provider and the transcript record; an idle timeout saves and closes, and what the role said it would do next is in the transcript, not in the engine. **Pace:** §7.2's figures are from one-file stages in the real lanes; the engine's own overhead is seconds and K1 removes the idle, but the model's time is the model's. **Autonomy:** `phase` lets a project spend up to its day budget with no human; the budgets and deadlines are the stop (D1 §13.3), and a run that spends to its deadline producing nothing is a Builder's failure the repair accounting catches, not something the engine prevents. **Impact analysis** is textual over ADRs (a key cited in an ADR's text), exact over requirements, checks, stages and evidence. **Ordering:** a stage integrated out of plan order by a parallel build is correct by the rebase, but the plan's `depends_on` is the Architect's word; an undeclared dependency shows as a failed check, not as a refusal.

### 12.2 Readings of the foundations settled here (no decision needed; each is a reading Sean may overrule)

1. **F §3.2 "blocking questions resolved" against TPL §10 "a spec with open questions can be approved".** Read together: an open question blocks approval only when it blocks a requirement the roadmap hint (TPL §5's phase column) places in phase 1; other open questions are carried, and the Architect may not plan a requirement whose question is open (TPL §10). §3.4 applies it as an option blocker.
2. **F §3.3's Developing gate ("task plan ... architecture approved") against F §3.10.6 ("a plan that passes ... proceeds without human approval").** The first phase plan is approved with the architecture, in one consolidated item (§3.4), because F §3.3 names the task plan in the entry gate; every later plan follows §3.10.6. No human act is added that F §3.3 did not have.
3. **F §3.10.5 "the Architect runs in a fresh session" against E10's `session` run kind.** "Fresh session" means fresh context from durable records, which a one-shot run gives by construction (D1 §15.3); an interactive session with no owner present would idle out. The replan is a one-shot (§5.6; Q2 asks Sean to confirm), the initial architecture a session.
4. **F §3.10.3 and §5.1 (checks before implementation, per phase) against F §4.1's Verifier ("approved requirements + fixed candidate → acceptance checks").** Both hold: the phase's checks are authored before its stages against the plan's interfaces (§6.2), and the Verifier of each candidate verifies against that fixed candidate; checks for interfaces that do not yet exist are added when they do (F §5.1's second paragraph).
5. **F §4.1 Vision and Spec Writer as two roles against SAP, which folds Vision into the Spec Writer's listen-and-reflect phases.** Two roles, two sessions, two prohibitions (§3.1, §3.2); the owner may skip Vision by creating the project with his own idea (F §3.2 "human creates or accepts"). Nothing in F requires a Vision session.
6. **F §3.10.2 "every approved requirement is traceable to its planned phase" against §3.8's added requirement.** An added requirement has no phase until a roadmap revision places it; meanwhile it is `REQUIREMENT_UNASSIGNED` on later phase gates (§4.5), visibly pending, never silently dropped.

8. **F §5.6 cadence at T1 ("once at candidate level") against E11's T1 nomination "at phase completion, or on the Builder's request".** §5.8 step 1 nominates at phase completion when the cadence has not; a T1 phase's stages therefore build back to back with one verification (§7.4).
