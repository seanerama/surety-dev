## 1. The journey

What M5 must prove first, end to end, on a scripted backend and then once for real (E40). **Two journeys** (E139 item 8): this one, from an idea the owner types to `alpha_deployed`, and §8's, from an existing repository adopted to the same end. One project here, from an idea the owner types to `alpha_deployed`. Each step names the human act, if any, and the engine record it leaves; the sections after say why each is safe. The project is T2 with two phases of two stages, the smallest shape that exercises a phase boundary and the Architect's re-entry.

1. **The owner creates the project with the idea:** `surety project create <name> --repo <path> --idea idea-brief.md` (IBT's form, or a paragraph). The engine bootstraps the repository as D1 §3.1 (journaled `.surety/project.json`), commits `.surety/idea.md` as the idea record version 1, and records the baseline `idea` (F §3.2: "human creates or accepts the idea"). `project.created`, `project.registered`, `baseline.idea_recorded`. No model.
2. **A Vision session** (§3.1), opened by `surety session open vision`: the owner and the role talk; each turn is one metered invocation (E10); the role rewrites `.surety/idea.md` into the project identity (§3.1); `surety session save` commits it as an `intent` revision; `surety session close` ends the run and raises `idea_accept` with the record as evidence. The owner accepts: idea record version 2, still baseline `idea`. **Optional:** an owner who wrote the brief himself skips this session (the human created the idea, F §3.2).
3. **A Spec Writer session** (§3.2): the role turns the idea record into `.surety/spec/spec.md` in TPL's shape; at every save the engine parses TPL §5's index (D3 §4.5) and tells the session what did not parse; close raises `spec_approval` with the parsed index, the open questions and the sensitive areas as observed evidence. **The owner approves** (F §9.1's floor): `spec_revisions` version 1, `requirements` registered with criteria and areas, `modules` from TPL §6 with their sensitive areas, the environments of TPL §7 recorded by name, baseline `spec_ready`. `baseline.spec_approved`.
4. **An Architect session** (§3.3): from the approved spec the role writes `.surety/architecture/architecture.md` and `modules.json`, `.surety/adrs/ADR-nnn.md`, `.surety/roadmap/roadmap.md` and `roadmap.json` (both phases, their goals, modules, requirements and dependencies), and `.surety/phases/phase-1.md` and `phase-1.json` (two stages with tasks, dependencies and validation scope). At close the engine runs the mechanical plan check (F §3.10.6; §5.3) against the **prospective bundle** (B02; §3.4: the approved spec and head, every proposed blob, and the versions publication will allocate) and raises `architecture_approval` consolidated with `plan_approval` for phase 1 (F §3.3: the Developing entry gate is "ADRs, task plan, and validation scope; architecture approved"). The session's saves were checkpoints: draft intent, no baseline, no stage, no work. **The owner approves.** The authorized finalizer publishes the bundle exactly once (§5.5): `architecture_revisions` 1, `modules` refined, `roadmap_revisions` 1, `phase_plans` 1 registered with its two `stages` and their `stage_build` work (`registerPlan`, as built, extended by K4). `baseline.architecture_approved`, `baseline.roadmap_revised`, `baseline.plan_approved`. The candidate lifecycle is now `developing` (F §3.3).
5. **Phase 1 opens with the Verifier's check authoring** (§6.2; F §3.10.3, §5.1): `check_authoring` work, one Verifier run, writes under `.surety/checks/` the definitions and programs for every criterion of phase 1's requirements, the `smoke` and `security_lint` kinds the tier needs (D3 §4.3), the floor checks of every sensitive area touched, and the `post_deploy_identity` and `post_deploy_behavior` checks TPL §7 specifies (D4 J4). The diff is captured as a protected proposal (D1 §7.3 step 2), classified by D3 §3.1 as computed (the first phase's names the toolchain in `check_commands`, so it is `unclassifiable` and goes to the owner, Q9), applied by a Reviewer run under `classifier_authority` `authoritative` or by the human under `recommend` (D3 §3.3). `protected.applied`; protected version 2 effective. **Only then is the phase ready for Builders** (B07; §6.2): the Verifier's run completing is not readiness; the applied protected version is. Before anything is built, the checks exist and fail.
6. **Stage 1** (§6.3): the engine dispatches a Builder (`stage_build` eligible, its dependencies none); the Builder builds in a sandboxed workspace, writes its result, the engine snapshots, validates and integrates (D1 §7.3, §7.5), nominates candidate c-1 (T2 cadence, F §5.6, E11), registers and runs its checks (D3 §2.5, no model), dispatches the Verifier (`verification` work) and then the Reviewer (`review` work), evaluates the `stage` gate, and on `satisfied` completes the stage's work. A failed check sends the Builder back (D3 §2.10); a finding dispositioned `fix` makes fix work (E43). **No human act** within the stage under the phase autonomy profile (§6.5), unless the foundations route one: a loosening or unclassifiable check correction, a Medium deferral, a blocker.
7. **Stage 2** follows on stage 1's integrated code (its `depends_on` is met when stage 1's active generation is `integrated`, not `verified`; B08); its Builder may start while stage 1's Verifier and Reviewer run (§6.4). Records as step 6, candidate c-2 superseding c-1 (D1 §3.3, as built).
8. **Phase verification** (§5.8; F §3.10.4): with both stages verified (this is T2; at T1 the trigger is the integrations and the authoring obligations complete, and the engine nominates then, B06), the engine evaluates the `phase` gate for phase 1 on the current candidate (its scope every requirement assigned to the phase, D3 §4.2, with the phase-scope checks and the regression set registered and run on that candidate), and dispatches `phase_verification` work for the Verifier (regressions in what the phase touched; its sign-off required at T2 and T3). Satisfied: `phase_plans.completed_at`, `baseline.phase_complete`, once for that binding. Phase completion establishes nothing about deployment (F §3.10.4).
9. **The Architect's re-entry** (§5.6; F §3.10.5): `replan` work, one Architect one-shot run with a context package built from durable records, never from the first session; it commits `phase-2.json` and `.md`, perhaps a roadmap revision, as an `intent` revision; the finalizer runs the mechanical check and **computes the routing from the artifacts** (B03; §5.4): the approved architecture, module map, ADRs and roadmap unchanged by diff and every check passing, phase 2 is published and registered with no human act (F §3.10.6). A proposal whose diff changes any of them, needs a spec change or declares itself uncertain raises `plan_approval` instead, with the complete diff.
10. **Phase 2** repeats steps 5 to 8 for its two stages; its check authoring adds the phase's checks as a tightening.
11. **A specification change, mid-build** (§4; F §3.8), because a real project has one: the owner submits `surety spec change-request --file cr-1.md`; a Spec Writer run writes the proposed diff on a side ref; the engine computes the impact (affected criteria, checks, ADRs, stages, evidence, bound to the semantic digests and the dependency generations, B05); `spec_change` is raised with the impact visible; **the owner approves** (the floor, as spec approval); spec version 2 is committed, the spec-evidence generation advances, the affected results are invalidated and their checks carry unresolved obligations that old-generation runs cannot clear, `check_correction` work is registered for the Verifier, and early replanning (F §3.10.7) is registered for the Architect when the change touches the current or a later phase.
12. **Alpha** (D4 §1): the owner configured the environment and qualified the adapter once (D4 steps 1 and 2; both can happen any time before this). With the last phase complete, the deployment request is made by the owner's command (D4 Q3) or by the engine under the project policy `alpha_deploy: on_phase_complete` (§6.8, decision Q5), **through the same production request path as the command** (B14): the engine seals the artifact, derives and evaluates `alpha_authorize` over the whole delivered requirement set, and only then creates authorized deploy work; it deploys, verifies by observation, and on `alpha_complete` moves the candidate `developing → alpha_deployed` (`candidate.advanced`). Requirements no phase delivered stay visibly pending (F §3.10.2, §3.10.4).

**Human acts the journey requires:** the project creation (1), the spec approval (3), the architecture and first-plan approval (4), the spec change approval (11) and, unless policy says otherwise, the deploy command (12). Four to five, and every one is on F §9.1's floor or on F §3.3's entry gate. The Vision session and the owner's conversation in steps 2 to 4 are human time by choice, not ceremony. **Everything else is the engine's, and no agent approves anything** (E12: no role emits a gate outcome).

## 2. Sessions: how a conversational role runs

**2.1 A session is a run of kind `session`** (E10; D1 §2.6, §3.2, §4.2, §15.2; A.2 `RunKind`, `SessionState`). It holds one role, one capability grant, one budget, one workspace and one run lease, and a sequence of **turns**, each its own invocation receipt, execution domain and ledger row (D1 §13.1: "Session finalization reconciles turn invocations and creates no row for the session itself"). The session states and transitions are D1 A.5's, unchanged: `allocated → turn_running ⇄ open_idle → saving → open_idle`, `open_idle | saving → closing → finalizing → ended`. Opening a session starts no process (D1 §15.2). **A turn becomes `open_idle` only when it is settled** (B01; `D6-S10`): launch closure and domain termination established (D2 §1.8, §3.2, §4.4), collection and workspace materialization at a durable terminal outcome (D2 §1.4, §2.5; `finishRun` reconciles them apart from termination), the accepted reply or the explicit failure recorded in the transcript, and the invocation's usage recorded in the ledger, an unknown remainder included (D1 §13; E7, E10). A terminated domain is not a settled turn, and an idle session owns no process because its last turn was settled. Cancellation and recovery can neither append a late reply, materialize late bytes nor charge one invocation twice into a successor turn: a turn interrupted before settlement ends `unknown` or `failed` on its own row, its bytes retained, and the next turn starts from the durable records as they stand. Each settlement advances the session's **idle generation** (A.3), the one value save, close and next-turn admission check (§2.4).

**2.2 A turn is a one-shot invocation with the conversation in its context package** (decision Q1, recommended (a)). The owner's message is written by the engine, verbatim and redacted (D1 §14.2), as the next entry of the session's **durable transcript** (§2.3) in the same transaction that moves the session `open_idle → turn_running`. The engine then builds the turn's context package (D2 §1.3): the role prompt (§3), the session's transcript so far, the current state of the role's artifact in the workspace (the workspace itself is mounted, D2 §2.3), the engine's own notes on the last save (parse errors, refused paths, §2.5) and the result schema; and dispatches the backend exactly as a one-shot run (D2 §1.1, §1.2: the same template, `--no-session-persistence`, a fresh volatile home). The role's reply is its structured result (`/surety/out/result.json`: `{reply, artifact_changed, questions_for_owner?, done?}`), accepted on exit class `clean` (D2 §1.4), appended to the transcript as the role's entry, and shown to the owner. **Provider-side continuation is not used:** no `--resume`, no restored provider files, no provider conversation state (D2 §1.7's rule applied to turns). Every turn therefore starts from durable records, which is what F §3.9.3 requires of a resumed session anyway, so resumption (§2.4) and an ordinary turn are the same construction. **Cost of this choice:** each turn re-sends the transcript; what the provider's cache absorbs is **unmeasured until the ledger records it** (N01): the ledger records `cached_in` apart from `billable_in` (D1 §13.2) so the benchmark sees it (§7.3), and D6 claims no saving before then. **Alternative** (Q1 (b)): D2 §1.8's design, provider files carried as a `provider_files` record and restored, with the backend's own `--resume`; fewer tokens, but a dependency on provider file formats, a continuation qualification no canary has established (D2 §4.1 `session_qualified` false; AR N04), and a transcript the engine would hold only as the provider wrote it.

**2.3 The transcript is an engine record.** `session_transcripts`: one `transcript` record per session (D1 A.2 `RecordKind`), append-only by turn, each entry `{turn, speaker: owner | role | engine, at, text_ref}` with the text in the record through the redactor; the raw backend stream of each turn is the turn's own `transcript` record as for any run. **Only engine transitions assign `speaker` and `turn`** (B01): the owner's entry is written by the turn's admission, the role's by the acceptance of its result, the engine's by the save's or parse's transition; a `speaker` field in a role's reply, or a transcript-shaped marker in an artifact, is role text and can create no `owner` or `engine` entry, on write or on reconstruction after a restore. Secrets the owner types are redacted by registered pattern only (D1 §14.2); §11 class C says what that cannot catch. Metering: each turn's ledger row names the provider, model requested and observed, billable and cached tokens and cost status (E16b; D1 §13); `GET /v1/projects/:p/runs/:r` shows a session's turns and spend live (E10: "the UI shows session spend live"; in M5 the CLI does).

**2.4 Save, close, idle, resume** (E10; D1 §15.2, §7.3, §7.4). **Save** (`surety session save`, `POST …/sessions/:r/save`) is admitted only at `open_idle`; it snapshots the workspace, validates the diff against the role's permitted paths (§2.5) and commits a `checkpoint` revision on the project's integration branch through the journal (D1 §7.4, §7.5), as an `intent` revision (D1 §7.8); `current_base` advances; a refused save leaves the workspace retained and tells the session why on the next turn. **A checkpoint is draft intent** (B02): it creates no effective baseline, no stage and no authoring work, and no context package of a later role resolves to it (§3.5). **One idle generation, one winner** (B01; `D6-S04`): save, close and the admission of the next turn each run one transactional compare-and-swap on the session's idle generation; a save and a turn cannot both acquire the same generation, so a save never runs beside a live process or a materialization in flight, and the loser is refused `session_generation_stale` (A.6), never queued. **Every next turn revalidates** the grant, the backend's qualification and the session's **cumulative budget** (D1 §13.3; E10) before dispatch; pending or unknown usage of an earlier turn is handled by D2's budget policy, never as zero. **Close** (`surety session close`) saves if the workspace differs from `current_base`, then enters `closing` and `endRun` (D1 §4.5); a role's `done: true` in its result proposes closing and the owner's command performs it. Closing raises the decision the role's product calls for (§3.4). **Idle timeout** (`session_idle_timeout`, default 20 m): the engine saves (as above; a refused save is recorded and the artifact stays in the retained workspace) and closes with outcome `timed_out`, reason `deadline` (D1 §4.1); nothing is lost that was in the workspace. **Budget exhaustion** admits no further turn and closes through the same protocol as idle closure (B01): save, or record the retained workspace when the save is refused, then `closing` and the end protocol, outcome `budget_exhausted` (D1 §13.3; E7); the decision the product calls for is raised only if the product parses. **Resume** (`surety session resume <run>`) is D1 §15.3: a new run linked by `parent_run`, same role, a fresh grant, the workspace at the last committed checkpoint (a retained but uncommitted workspace is offered to the owner as `stash` or `discard` through the checkout integrity route, D1 §7.6, never silently adopted), and the transcript continued from the durable record; since every turn builds from durable records (§2.2), nothing is reconstructed that was not already there.

**2.5 What a session may write.** The role's permitted paths are its F §4.1 outputs and nothing else, enforced at save by D1 §7.3 step 1 (a diff outside them is `diff_violation`; the save is refused whole, the session is told, the workspace keeps the change for the owner to see): Vision `.surety/idea.md`; Spec Writer `.surety/spec/`; Architect `.surety/architecture/`, `.surety/adrs/`, `.surety/roadmap/`, `.surety/phases/`. None may touch source, `.surety/checks/` (protected, D1 §5.2) or `.surety/policy.json`. The engine, not the role, performs every commit (E2). A session's workspace is the project's repository at `current_base`, so the Architect reads the spec and the Spec Writer reads the idea without a copy.

**2.6 Qualification.** A session needs no provider continuation (§2.2), so no `session_headless` trust mode is activated (D2 A.2 stays reserved). What E10 asks to be qualified separately is established by one **continuation canary** added to D2 §7.2's attempt: two turns on the fixture project, the second instructed to answer from the first turn's transcript and artifact and seen to do so, with the first turn's domain terminated with closure before the second is dispatched (D2 §4.4's quiescence) and no provider file carried between them (inventoried, D2 §4.3). The entry records `session_qualified` true from that canary only; a one-shot entry without it refuses `session` runs with `backend_refused` (as built). Cost: one more paid canary per attempt, about the cost of the positive canary (M4: 0.05 USD for two). **The profile is part of what is qualified** (B09; `D6-X09`): a sandbox profile's memory and writable-storage bounds (§6.4's `role_memory_max`, `check_memory_max`, and D2's volatile storage charged to the same limit) are mutually valid and included in its qualification identity (D2 §4.1, §7.2); a changed bound is a changed profile, which follows D2's qualification and revocation protocol before any real dispatch under it. M2's qualification of the 8 GiB profile does not qualify the 4 GiB one; the real lane's attempt qualifies the profiles it runs.

**2.7 The client.** The CLI is a thin client (D1 §11.6): `surety session open <role> [--model <m>]`, `say <text>` or an interactive loop reading lines, `save`, `close`, `resume <run>`, `show <run>` (transcript and spend). Routes: `POST /v1/projects/:p/sessions {role, model?}`, `POST …/sessions/:r/turns {text}` (long-poll or SSE on `…/runs/:r/tail` for the reply), `…/save`, `…/close`, `…/resume`, `GET …/sessions/:r`; open, turn, save and close exist and are refused today (`src/api/server.ts`, `unsupported('Agent sessions')`), so M5 lifts those refusals; **show and resume are new routes** (N03). One open session per project at a time, and a session admits no one-shot run beside it on its project while a one-shot run holds a session's opening (`session_busy`; `D6-S08`): there is no run counter behind this (K2), only the project's session exclusivity, because both write the same branch and the integration lease serializes them anyway (D1 §7.5).

## 3. The three interactive roles and their products

Each role's prompt is engine-authored fixed text (D2 §1.2), the SAP's five phases (listen, reflect, explore, shape, document) recast per role, with the prohibitions of F §4.1 and the shape of its product. The prompts are M5's build artefacts; D6 fixes what each product is and how the engine reads it.

**3.1 Vision → the idea record** (F §4.1: "Conversation → idea record"; may not write spec, architecture, code or checks). Product: `.surety/idea.md`, IBT's sections filled, plus a closing **identity block** the engine parses: `name`, `one_line`, `users`, `non_goals`, `success_measures`, `sensitive_areas` (from F §5.6's closed list or `none`), `tier_recommendation` (T1, T2, T3 or `unsure`), `environments` (names). Everything else is prose for the Spec Writer and the owner. The engine reads the identity block at save and reports a block that does not parse. Approval: `idea_accept` (D1 A.8, not floor): the owner accepts, and the record's version is recorded on `projects.idea_version`; a project may be created with the idea and no session (§1 step 1).

**3.2 Spec Writer → the specification** (F §4.1: "Idea, change requests → versioned spec with acceptance criteria; proposed diffs"; may not approve its own spec, write architecture, code or checks). Product: `.surety/spec/spec.md` in TPL's exact shape: the header (`Spec version`, `Status: draft`, `Tier`, `Owner`), §4 one subsection per requirement `R<n>` with statement, rationale, sensitive areas and criteria `R<n>.<m>`, §5 the index table the engine parses (D3 §4.5; `src/checks/requirement-index.ts`), §6 modules and protected paths, §7 environments with D4's fields (target, runtime and start command, port variable, `artifact.exclude`, the required post-deploy behaviour check, stable arguments), §8 to §11. **What the engine reads and checks at every save** (B04; `D6-W05`): it builds the **semantic representation** of the specification, versioned with the save, from the exact syntax Appendix B.2 declares and from nothing else: the header; §5's index (a row that does not parse refuses the save's acceptance as spec content and names the row, as D3 §4.5 refuses approval); every requirement's statement and each criterion's identity **and text** from §4's subsections; sensitive areas; the `human-test` and descriptive marks; §10's open questions with the requirement keys each blocks; §6's modules; §7's environment proposals. Prose outside B.2's syntax is not claimed as mechanically interpreted. Checks on the representation: every `R<n>` of §4 has a row and every row a subsection; no duplicate requirement or criterion identity, and no contradiction between the index and the subsection (a criterion listed in one and absent from the other); each requirement has at least one criterion or is marked `human-test` in its subsection (a requirement with no criterion is uncertain and cannot be validated, D3 §4.3; the engine says so); sensitive areas are from the closed list; the tier is one of three; keys are never renumbered against the previous saved version (TPL: keys never change once confirmed; a key that disappears is `withdrawn`, not deleted). Reordering identified requirements or criteria without changing their values changes the representation's digest by nothing (`semantic_digest`, A.3). An unreadable, duplicate or contradictory representation refuses approval (§3.4), never approval of a default. The findings are given to the session as the engine's entry on the next turn. The Spec Writer reads the idea record and the project's constraints; in a change request (§4) it reads the current approved spec and the request.

**3.3 Architect → architecture, ADRs, module map, roadmap, phase plan** (F §4.1: "Approved spec → ADRs, architecture, diagrams, module map, phase roadmap, detailed phase plans, proposed validation scope"; may not approve its own proposals, change an approved requirement silently, write code or acceptance checks). Products, each prose beside a machine file the engine parses (Appendix B gives the schemas):
- `.surety/architecture/architecture.md`: the architecture, its diagrams as text; `modules.json`: the module map, each `{name, paths, sensitive_areas, tier_override?, interfaces: [{name, kind, path?, description}]}`, refining TPL §6 (O6: the Architect defines modules and maps features to them). Every TPL §6 module must be kept or explicitly split or renamed with the mapping stated; a sensitive area on a TPL module can be moved but never dropped (F §5.6's floor is the spec's).
- `.surety/adrs/ADR-<nnn>.md`: one decision each, with `key`, `status`, `context`, `decision`, `consequences`; the texts the context packages carry (as built, `baseline_texts` kind `adr`).
- `.surety/roadmap/roadmap.json`: `{version, architecture_version, phases: [{number, goal, modules, requirement_ids, depends_on}]}` with `roadmap.md` beside it (F §3.10.1). Every approved requirement appears in exactly one phase (F §3.10.2: "every approved requirement is traceable to its planned phase"); the check refuses a roadmap that leaves one out or lists one twice.
- `.surety/phases/phase-<n>.json`: `{phase, prepared_against: {spec_version, architecture_version, roadmap_version, revision}, stages: [{number, goal, tasks: [text], modules, implements, adrs, depends_on: [stage numbers], validation: {criteria: [R<n>.<m>], kinds: [CheckKind], sensitive_areas}, integration_obligations: [text]}], conformance: within_baseline | uncertain, notes}` with `phase-<n>.md` beside it (F §3.10.1: build stages with tasks, validation scope and integration obligations; default 3 to 5 stages, `phase_size`). The validation block is the "proposed validation scope" of F §4.1 and the input of F §3.10.6's fourth check; it proposes, and the Verifier's checks and D3 §4.2's required set decide.

**3.4 How each product is approved** (F §3.2, §3.3, §9.1, §9.2; E9's inventory). Closing a session raises the decision its role's product calls for, with the parsed machine files as `observed` evidence and the prose as `claimed` (D1 §3.4 `Provenance`), and the session's run ends; the decision is answered later, from the queue, like any other (D1 §10). **Vision:** `idea_accept`, not floor. **Spec Writer:** `spec_approval`, **floor** (F §9.1); the option `approve` is blocked (D1 §9.7's blockers on the option) while the semantic representation is unreadable, duplicate or contradictory (B04), a requirement has no criterion and no `human-test` mark, or an open question of TPL §10 blocks a requirement the roadmap hint assigns to phase 1 or **a requirement or question with no phase hint at all** (§12.2 reading 1; B04: an unassigned question cannot make approval vacuously safe; the Architect may not plan a requirement whose question is open, §5.3 clause 1); in retrofit the mode rules of §8.1 step 2 apply explicitly (B10). The decision's manifest binds the parsed semantics and the blobs, not the index's identities alone. Its effect: `spec_revisions` row with its `semantic_digest`, the requirements and criteria with their text (`registerRequirementIndex` as built, extended to the representation), `ensureModules` from TPL §6, environments by name (a baseline operation with no adapter, as the fixture helper `configureEnvironment` records them today; "nothing is ever deployed to it" until D4's configuration, which this is not, N03), `baseline_state` `spec_ready`, the change log row written by the engine. **Architect:** `architecture_approval` for the architecture, module map and ADRs, consolidated (D1 §10.3, one `batch_key`) with `plan_approval` for phase 1, raised only after the mechanical check passed (a failing check returns to the session as the engine's entry, and close is refused until it passes or the owner closes with `--refuse`, which records the products and raises nothing). **The approval binds one prospective bundle** (B02; `D6-A06`): the current approved spec version and integration head, every proposed artifact blob (architecture, module map, ADRs, roadmap, phase plan), and the versions the publication will allocate (`architecture_revisions` n, `roadmap_revisions` n, `phase_plans` for phase 1); the engine checks the bundle (§5.3 against those prospective versions, so the first plan can name versions that do not yet exist) before it accepts the approval; a save after the preview changes the blobs and stales the decision (D1 §4.6). Both decisions are human in M5; the automated prototype policy of F §9.2 is not built (§11 class B). Effect: the authorized finalizer **publishes the bundle exactly once** (§5.5): `architecture_revisions`, `modules` replaced by the map, `roadmap_revisions` 1, `phase_plans` 1 with `registerPlan` (K4), stages and their work; an unapproved or refused checkpoint cannot invoke that finalizer. A later Architect **session** opened by the owner on an existing architecture (to revise an ADR or the roadmap) raises `architecture_approval` again on close with the diff shown; the engine's impact (§4.4's function over ADRs and roadmap) is its evidence, and its publication follows the same protocol.

**3.5 Context packages** (F §3.10.8; D2 §1.3). **Every production package resolves approved baseline artifacts by their approved immutable bindings** (B02; `D6-A01`): the spec by `spec_revisions.git_blob`, ADR and requirement text by the `baseline_texts` of the approved revision, the roadmap and plan by their published blobs; a newer checkpoint of the same file in the checkout cannot replace them, and the texts the packages carry are refreshed at each approval (N03: the M5 seam reconciles `baseline_texts` with the versions). Vision: the idea record and IBT. Spec Writer: the accepted idea record, TPL, the sensitive-area list, the tier table (F §5.7 in the engine's words), and for a change request the approved spec and the request. Architect (session): the approved spec with its index, TPL §6 and §7, the foundations' planning rules in the engine's words (phase size, one phase at a time, every requirement assigned, validation per criterion), Appendix B's schemas, and the repository as it is. Architect (replan, §5.6): the current spec and index, every ADR, the current roadmap and module map, the completed phase's plan with each stage's `integrated_revision`, the phase gate's evaluation and the per-criterion check states, every finding of the phase with its disposition, the deferrals outstanding, the open objections, and the repository at the integration head; never the first session's transcript (F §3.10.5 step 2).

## 4. The specification as a governed artifact

**4.1 The baseline.** `spec_revisions` (D1 A.3): `version`, `git_path` `.surety/spec/spec.md`, `git_blob`, `semantic_digest` (B04; the digest of §3.2's representation), `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `supersedes`. Approval is the only writer; a checkpoint writes nothing here (B02); the blob it names is the one committed at the session's last save, and the approval's effect plan (D1 §9.7) names that blob and that digest, so a save between preview and answer stales the decision. The project carries a **spec-evidence generation** (`projects.spec_evidence_generation`, A.3; B05) that every spec approval advances and every check registration records. `requirements` rows carry `spec_revision`, `key`, `text` (as built), `criteria`, `sensitive_areas`, `assigned_phase` (from the roadmap, §5.1), `status` (`pending` until a gate evaluation verifies its criteria, D1 §3.1; `withdrawn` is added to `RequirementStatus`, A.2). A requirement's key never changes (TPL §4); a withdrawn one keeps its row.

**4.2 The change request** (F §3.8 step 1). `POST /v1/projects/:p/spec/change-requests {text | file}` (`surety spec change-request`; the route D1 §11.4 names) by the owner, or by the engine from a `requirement_conflict` finding's `change_spec` answer (D3 §5 X2). It records a `spec_change_requests` row `{seq, spec_revision (current), text →records, source: owner | finding →findings, status}` and creates `spec_change` work (D1 A.2; K8 gives it a run). **One proposal at a time per project** (B05; `D6-C01`): one request is dispatched and at most one proposal awaits its decision; the others wait `eligible` until the pending one is approved or rejected, so every impact is computed against one current version and no second proposal is approved while the first awaits an answer. (The alternative, every proposal independently rebound and rechecked at approval, is not built.)

**4.3 The proposed diff** (F §3.8 step 2). The `spec_change` work's run is a Spec Writer one-shot (or, on the owner's `--session`, a session) whose workspace is the repository at the integration head; it may write `.surety/spec/` only; its result names the changed requirements and the reason per change. The snapshot is validated (D1 §7.3) and committed, **not to the integration branch**, but as an `intent` revision on a registered side ref `refs/surety/spec-change/<seq>` (D1 §7.2 `RefKind` gains `spec_change`, A.2), journaled like any ref write; the proposal binds that revision's blob. Nothing of the current spec changes yet.

**4.4 Impact analysis** (F §3.8 step 3), computed by the engine from durable records and the two semantic representations (§3.2), recorded as a `spec_impact` record (provenance `observed`) and as the decision's evidence. **What it binds** (B05; `D6-C04`): the approved and the proposed `semantic_digest`, and the current **dependency generations** of plans, requirements, checks, findings, results and work (the dependency manifest, A.3); preview and effect recompute the manifest, and a material change between them (a new result, finding, work status or plan generation touching an affected subject) makes the answer stale. **What it lists:**
- **Requirements:** per key, `added`, `withdrawn`, `text_changed` (the statement differs), `criteria_added`, `criteria_removed`, `criteria_changed` (a criterion's text differs, its ID unchanged), `areas_changed`; a key renumbered is a refusal of the diff (`spec_keys_renumbered`), not an impact. **A requirement-statement change affects its criteria and their checks even when every ID remains** (B05): the criteria of a `text_changed` requirement are listed as affected. Reordering requirements or criteria without changing their values is no impact (`D6-C03`).
- **Checks** (D3 §1.4, §3.4): every check of the effective protected version whose `covers.criteria` names a removed, changed or affected criterion, and every added criterion with no check (an uncovered criterion, `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` to come). A check on a removed criterion will be a discovery error `criterion_unknown` after approval; a check on a changed or affected criterion keeps a valid ID and is therefore **listed here as an unresolved obligation** (§4.5), because discovery cannot see a changed meaning.
- **ADRs and architecture:** ADRs whose text cites an affected key (a textual reference `R<n>` in the ADR's body), reported as that dependency class, textual, not as semantic analysis (B05); modules whose sensitive areas the change alters, reported as the structural class. Where semantic independence is not established (a requirement whose statement changed and whose module no ADR cites), the affected scope is the conservative one, every check of the requirement's criteria, and the uncertainty is shown to the approver as such.
- **Stages and roadmap:** stages whose `implements` includes an affected requirement, with their status; the phases of the roadmap holding affected or added requirements; whether the change touches the current phase (early replanning, F §3.10.7) or only later ones.
- **Evidence:** every `check_results` row whose check covers an affected criterion, and every gate evaluation that selected one, listed as to be invalidated; findings naming an affected criterion.
- **Work:** open or running work items touching affected stages.
Nothing is estimated: an impact the engine cannot compute (an unreadable blob, an unparsable index) refuses the proposal with the reason, never an empty impact.

**4.5 Approval and invalidation** (F §3.8 steps 4 and 5; floor, F §9.1 "human approval of the project spec"). `spec_change` is raised with the impact and the diff; its options are `approve` and `reject`; its manifest binds the current spec version, the side ref's revision, the effective protected version and the impact's hash, so any change stales it (D1 §4.6). On `approve`, one effect: the side revision is integrated onto the integration branch through the journal (D1 §7.5, a rebase if the head moved, the spec files alone changing); `spec_revisions` version n+1 with `supersedes` and its `semantic_digest`; requirements re-registered (keys kept; withdrawn marked; `assigned_phase` kept where the roadmap still names the key, null for an added one); the change log row appended by the engine; `baseline.spec_approved` with `version`. Then, in the same finalizer (B05; `D6-C05`): **the spec-evidence generation advances**; every listed result invalidated (`invalidated_at`, reason `spec_changed`), every listed evaluation stale; the effective protected version's discovery errors recomputed (D3 §3.4); **an unresolved obligation recorded for every listed check** (`check_obligations`, A.3: the check, the affected criteria, the new generation, reason `spec_changed`) so every gate carries `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` naming the check until the obligation is resolved; `check_correction` work registered for the Verifier naming the affected and uncovered criteria (D3 §2.11's route); and `replan` work registered when the impact touches the current or a later phase (F §3.10.7, with the impact as its triggering evidence); an added requirement with no phase blocks the `phase` gate of every later phase (`REQUIREMENT_UNASSIGNED`) until a roadmap revision places it. **Old-generation work provides no current acceptance:** a check run queued or running at approval carries the generation it was registered under; its result is recorded as history (`generation` on the row) and satisfies no current gate and clears no obligation, whether it finishes `passed` or not; the runner re-registers the affected checks under the new generation. **What resolves an obligation:** a protected correction applied (D3 §3) that names the criteria, or the Verifier's explicit assessment on the correction item that the check applies unchanged to the changed meaning, recorded with provenance; in either case fresh deciding evidence under the current generation is still required. Rediscovery of an unchanged criterion ID clears nothing. **A cancelled correction is registered again by the owner** (E174 item 3; approved by Sean, 2026-10-20). The trigger: a `spec_obligation` correction parks and its blocker is answered `cancel`, after which nothing else registers one. `surety spec correct --project P --request R` (`POST /v1/projects/:p/spec/change-requests/:r/correct`) registers it again at the next generation, with the same request, criteria and checks. It is refused `409 correction_pending` while a correction of the request is neither complete nor cancelled, `409 nothing_owed` when the request has no open obligation, and `404` for an unknown project or request. The blocker's question and its `cancel` consequence name the command with the real ids. `GET …/spec` lists each change request with open obligations, with a `next_action` naming the command. On `reject`, the side ref is kept as history (`keep`), the request `rejected`. **Evidence reuse across a spec change is not automatic** (F §3.8): the Verifier's explicit assessment above is the one assessment D6 creates, and it covers applicability, never the result; the runner re-runs (D3 §3.5), which costs no model.

**4.6 How a change flows to stages, checks and gates.** A stage whose requirement was withdrawn keeps its history; if it is `planned`, the replan removes or retargets it; if `building`, its run completes and the replan decides; if `integrated` or `verified`, nothing is undone (source changes are new candidates, F §3.8's table). A removed criterion makes its check `criterion_unknown` at discovery; a changed or affected criterion with an unchanged ID leaves its check with an unresolved obligation (§4.5); either way every gate is `ACCEPTANCE_SCOPE_INCOMPLETE` naming the check until the Verifier's correction lands as a protected proposal (tightening or otherwise, D3 §3) or the explicit assessment is recorded, and fresh evidence under the new generation decides; the stage gate of an already-complete stage is not re-evaluated for completion (the work is terminal), but every later gate, `phase` and `alpha_authorize` included, sees the new version (D1 §9.1: scope from the candidate and the approved bindings as they are now). A candidate nominated before the change keeps `spec_revision` as a historical fact (D1 A.3); its evaluations under the new version are new evaluations.

**4.7 What is not a spec change.** Moving a requirement between phases is a roadmap revision (F §3.10.2), by the Architect's replan or a session, recorded `baseline.roadmap_revised` with the move visible; it changes `assigned_phase` and nothing else. A module split or an ADR change is an architecture revision (§3.4). Neither touches `spec_revisions`.

## 5. Planning

**5.1 The roadmap** (F §3.10.1, §3.10.2). `roadmap_revisions` (D1 A.3): `version`, `architecture_revision`, `git_path`, `phases` from `roadmap.json`. Written by the Architect's session (version 1) or a replan (a revision); every version is recorded, none edited. The engine derives `requirements.assigned_phase` from the current version, and the project read shows each requirement with its phase and its delivery status per candidate (D1 §9.1, computed; F §3.10.2 "visibly pending"). Phase dependencies must be acyclic and each phase's modules a subset of the module map.

**5.2 The phase plan** (F §3.10.1). `phase_plans`: `roadmap_revision`, `phase_number`, `prepared_against_spec`, `prepared_against_architecture` (added, A.3), `prepared_against_revision`, `git_path`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, `completed_at` (added), `verification →gate_evaluations?` (added). Exactly one plan is open per project: the next phase's, produced only after the previous phase is complete (F §3.10.1 "produced only for the next phase"); an attempt to register a plan for a later phase is refused (`phase_not_next`); a second plan for the open phase is refused, **except through the early-replacement route of §5.7**, which is a compare-and-swap on the plan's **active generation** (`phase_plans.generation`, A.3; B08). Stages (`stages`, as built, plus K4's `depends_on`, `tasks`, `validation`, `integration_obligations`) number 3 to 5 by default, `phase_size` `{min, max}` per project (F §9.2); a plan outside the bound is `uncertain` and goes to the human, never refused outright, because a two-stage phase at the end of a project is sense, not error.

**5.3 The mechanical check** (F §3.10.6; `checkPlan(bundle)`, a pure function of the proposed artifact tree, the bundle it is checked against (§3.4: approved or prospective) and the store, recorded as a `plan_check` record and on the plan row). Each clause yields a reason code (A.4) with its subjects; a plan with any reason is `refused` (`baseline.plan_refused`) and returned to its author:
1. **References.** Every `implements` key is a requirement of the bundle's spec revision and not withdrawn (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_REQUIREMENT`); every module is in the bundle's module map (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_MODULE`); every ADR key exists under `.surety/adrs/` in the proposed tree (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_ADR`); every `validation.criteria` key is a criterion of a requirement the stage implements (`PLAN_CRITERION_MISMATCH`); every `depends_on` names a stage of this plan (`PLAN_UNKNOWN_STAGE`); no two stages share a number (`PLAN_DUPLICATE_STAGE`); **no stage implements a requirement an unresolved TPL §10 question blocks** (`PLAN_QUESTION_OPEN`; B04, `D6-P07`), and a question or requirement with no phase hint does not escape this clause by having none: it blocks every plan until the question is resolved or the requirement is assigned.
2. **Approvals.** `prepared_against` equals the bundle's spec, architecture and roadmap versions and the integration head (`PLAN_STALE_BASELINE`); a replan prepared against a head that moved by an engine commit since is re-based mechanically (the plan's files are intent artifacts), its `prepared_against_revision` updated, **and rechecked and rerouted against the new bundle** (B03): harmless metadata movement (a checkpoint, a protected application) and semantically material movement (a new approved spec or roadmap version) are both recomputed, and the second refuses; one prepared against an older spec version is refused.
3. **Dependencies.** The stage graph is acyclic (`PLAN_CYCLE`); the phase's stages touch only modules the roadmap lists for the phase (`PLAN_MODULE_OUTSIDE_PHASE`); every requirement the roadmap assigns to the phase is implemented by some stage of the plan, or the plan carries a roadmap revision moving it (`PLAN_REQUIREMENT_UNPLANNED`); every phase the roadmap says this one depends on is complete (`PLAN_DEPENDENCY_INCOMPLETE`).
4. **Validation obligations** (B04; `D6-P04`). The check records **planned obligations at their applicable scopes**: for each stage and for the phase, the criteria, the effective tier (the module's `tier_override` raising it), the kinds and the sensitive areas that scope must validate, derived from the stage's `implements` and `modules` and the phase's assignment, not from the union of the three `validation` arrays. Every criterion of every requirement assigned to the phase is in the `validation.criteria` of a stage that implements it (`PLAN_CRITERION_UNVALIDATED`); every sensitive area of those requirements and of the phase's modules is in the `validation.sensitive_areas` of a stage touching that module (`PLAN_AREA_UNVALIDATED`); the kinds F §5.7 requires at each scope's effective tier appear in that scope (`PLAN_KIND_MISSING`). The function is D3 §4.3's coverage primitive (`computeScope`'s counting) given an **explicit planned-obligation input** in place of the required set, as D3 §4.3 anticipated: planned entries are never fabricated `CheckDefinition`s and never acceptance evidence; check authoring (§6.2) must later establish the actual protected required set against the recorded obligations, and the phase gate (§5.8) compares the two.

**5.4 Routing** (F §3.10.6 "approval routing"; B03; `D6-P05`). **Routing is computed from the proposed artifact tree against the bundle,** never from the Architect's declarations: the engine compares every architecture and module-map artifact (text and `modules.json`), every ADR added, changed or deleted, every roadmap phase added or removed, every phase goal, membership, requirement assignment and dependency, and the proposed plan, and records the differences on the `plan_check` (`changes_architecture`, `changes_adrs`, `changes_roadmap`, each with its subjects). A role's declaration (`conformance: uncertain`, `notes`) can **add** uncertainty and route to the human; it can never suppress a detected change. **Automatic registration** is limited to a structurally valid next-phase plan whose approved architecture, module map, ADRs and roadmap are **unchanged by the diff** and whose §5.3 checks all pass: `baseline.plan_approved`, `approved_by` `engine:plan_check`, published by §5.5. **Every baseline-changing proposal** (any detected change above, or `uncertain`) raises `plan_approval` (D1 A.8; not floor) with the check's record and the **complete diff** of every changed artifact as evidence; the human's `approve` publishes it, `reject` returns it to the Architect as a new `replan` with the note. **An unknown requirement** (an `implements` key the spec lacks) is refused by §5.3 clause 1 and offered through the spec-change workflow (§4.2), never approved as an invalid plan "needing a spec change"; a `requirement_conflict` the Architect reports in its result (a requirement it cannot plan) is a finding routed to `change_spec` (D3 X2), never a silent change (F §4.1). Mechanical conformance establishes these declared structural properties, not the semantic adequacy of a stage's task prose (§11 class C).

**5.5 Registration** (D1 §7.8; `registerPlan`, K4; B02; `D6-P06`). **Publication is the authorized finalizer's act**, once, and only after the authority for the bundle exists: the owner's `approve` on the consolidated or `plan_approval` decision, or §5.4's automatic routing on a replan's commit. The finalizer compares the bundle it was authorized for with the current baseline and the plan's active generation **again** at publication; a mismatch (a competing proposal published first, a baseline advanced since) refuses with `plan_generation_stale` (A.6) and the proposal is stale, never current. It then writes, atomically with the commit's confirmation: the published versions (`architecture_revisions`, `roadmap_revisions` where the bundle carries them); the `phase_plans` row with its `generation`; the `stages` rows with `depends_on` resolved to stage ids and `status` `planned`; one `stage_build` work item per stage from trigger `(plan, <stage>, 1)`, with `depends_on` the stage-code milestone of the stages it depends on (§6.4) **and** the phase's authoring readiness (§6.2); the `check_authoring` work itself, trigger `(phase_plan, <plan>, 1)`. A session's checkpoint commit invokes none of this (B02): `finalizeIntegration`'s present call of `registerPlan` for supplied plans gains the authority check, and an unapproved or refused checkpoint registers nothing. Recovery after a crash between the commit and the finalizer produces the authorized publication once or a stale proposal, never runnable work from draft intent and never a duplicate. A published plan is schedulable by construction and nothing else can create stage work (CH incident 11).

**5.6 Re-entry after each phase** (F §3.10.5). When the `phase` gate is satisfied and `phase_verification` work complete (§5.8), the finalizer records `completed_at` and creates `replan` work, trigger `(phase_complete, <plan>, 1)`, if the roadmap has a next phase. Its run is an Architect **one-shot** (decision Q2, recommended (a)): deadline `deadline_architect` (30 m), the context package of §3.5, permitted paths as §2.5, result `{plan: phase-<n+1>, roadmap_revision?: ..., adrs_changed?: [...], conformance, notes}`. Its commit is an `intent` revision on the integration branch whose finalizer runs §5.3 and §5.4 and publishes only through §5.5's protocol (automatic registration or `plan_approval`, the same check and publication without a human answer when routing permits, B02). For an adopted project the replan after phase 1 is created only when §8.3's Alpha barrier is open (B11). A refused plan ends the run `failed` with `invalid_result` naming the reasons; the `replan` item takes one automatic repair with the reasons in its package (D1 §4.3's repair accounting, `repair_attempts_max`), then parks with a blocker for the owner, who may open an Architect session on it instead. When the roadmap has no next phase, the project's build is complete and §6.8 applies.

**5.7 Early replanning** (F §3.10.7). `replan` work is also created, with the triggering evidence on its trigger, by: an approved spec change touching the current or a later phase (§4.5); a blocker on a stage's work parked at `repair_attempts_max` or `no_progress_max` whose decision the owner answers `replan` (a new option on `blocker` for stage work, A.2); an objection assessed and upheld as being against the plan (the `blocker` of D3 X2 answered `replan`); a `contract_conflict` finding the Reviewer raises against the plan with disposition `fix` scoped to the plan. Filing alone triggers nothing (F §3.10.7). **Early replacement is an authorized compare-and-swap on the active plan generation** (B08; `D6-F04`), the one route exempt from §5.2's second-plan refusal, and it goes through §5.3 to §5.5 like any plan: the replacement names the generation it replaces; it **preserves completed stage identities and history** (stages `integrated` or `verified` keep their rows, revisions, findings and work); it **cancels obsolete unbuilt obligations** (stages `planned` are `cancelled` with their work, `work.cancelled`, `cancelled_at`); and it **registers only the replacement remainder**, never a duplicate of a kept stage. A stage `building` finishes its run; its result is **retained for the replacement only when every execution and acceptance input is unchanged**: number, goal, `implements`, `tasks`, `modules`, `adrs`, the identities of its `depends_on`, `validation` and `integration_obligations`; otherwise its late integration is history (the code stays as working revisions) and fulfils no replacement work. Delivery (D1 §9.1), dispatch and completion use the **active obligation set**; cancellation erases no finding against delivered code and lowers no sensitivity floor. Two concurrent replacements: one wins the swap, the other is `plan_generation_stale`; recovery cannot register the remainder twice. The new plan's `prepared_against_revision` is the integration head. Every early replan records which tasks, assumptions and checks became stale, from the impact of §4.4 or the blocker's evidence (F §3.10.7).

**5.8 Phase verification and the `phase` gate** (F §3.10.4, §5.6 cadence; D3 §4.2, §4.3, which defined the scope and left the gate `unsupported`; B06; `D6-F01` to `D6-F03`). **The trigger differs by tier**, because at T1 the stages' verification depends on the nomination this step makes (E11 "at phase completion"; reading 8). **At T1:** when every stage of the open plan is `integrated` and the phase's authoring obligations are complete (§6.2), the engine nominates the current integration revision (`engine_cadence`), **registers and executes the required checks for the held stages and for the phase scope** on that candidate, phase-only checks (a check whose `gate_kinds` lists `phase` alone; `SCOPE_KINDS` gains `phase`, K5) and the declared regression set included, dispatches `verification` work, and then evaluates the `stage` gates of the held stages and the `phase` gate on that candidate. **At T2 and T3:** stage nominations stay per stage (§6.3); when every stage of the open plan is `verified`, phase completion registers the additional phase obligations (phase-only checks, the regression set) on the current candidate, runs them, and evaluates `phase`. **The `phase` gate's scope** on either path: every requirement assigned to the phase, delivered or not (D3 §4.2 (b)); the regression set, every required check of every stage of this and earlier phases at the scope's tier, plus the floor, **executed on this candidate**: historical stage verification is not current regression evidence. **`phase_verification` work** is registered when the phase checks have run: a Verifier run that reads the evaluation and the phase's findings, may raise findings and propose corrections, and **signs off the phase at T2 and T3; the sign-off is not required at T1** (`signoffs.scope` gains `phase`, A.2). Its binding is the candidate's acceptance content hash, the effective protected version, the spec revision and the phase plan's generation; supersession transfers nothing, and a sign-off bound to a replaced plan or candidate is refused. **Completion:** when the gate is satisfied and, at T2 and T3, the sign-off is recorded, the engine marks the plan `completed_at` and every stage of it `verified`, emits `baseline.phase_complete` and creates the `replan` (§5.6), **once for that binding** (`phase_plans.completion_binding`, A.3): repeated ticks, a restart or a later regression create no second completion and no second replan, and a later regression changes no history; it blocks current effects (the deploy request, the next plan's dependency clause) through the gates that reread. A `phase` gate not satisfied names its reasons like any gate; a requirement assigned to the phase with no delivering stage is `PLAN_REQUIREMENT_UNPLANNED` on the gate too; planned obligations with no actual protected check are `AUTHORING_INCOMPLETE` (§6.2). **Ordinary T1 integrations claim no check execution** unless they cause an explicit registration (the Builder's nomination request, E11). **Phase completion establishes nothing about deployment:** `alpha_authorize` keeps its own scope over the whole delivered set (F §3.10.4).

## 6. The build's execution

**6.1 What M3 and M4 built and M5 keeps unchanged.** The Builder run in a `role` domain with a scoped context package (D2); snapshot, validation, integration through the journal (D1 §7.3, §7.5); nomination by cadence (E11; D3 L3); check registration and the runner (D3 §2); the Verifier and Reviewer runs, findings, dispositions, repairs and the fix loop (E43; D3 §2.10, §2.11); the protected update path and the classifier (D3 §3); the stage gate and `alpha_authorize` (D1 §9); the deployment, verification and `alpha_complete` (D4). M5 adds what makes them run **in sequence over many stages and phases without a hand on the wheel**: dependencies and ordering (§6.4), the autonomy profile (§6.5), check authoring per phase (§6.2), the phase gate and replan (§5), the deploy request policy (§6.8), and the pace instruments (§7.6).

**6.2 Check authoring per phase** (F §3.10.3, §5.1; decision settled, §12.2 reading 4). `check_authoring` work (A.2, new kind; transitions as `check_correction`) is registered with each phase plan (§5.5) and runs before any stage of the phase: a Verifier run whose package is `src/checks/guide.ts`'s (the governed values, the index, the stages of the plan with their `implements` and `validation`, the environments, D3 Appendix B) plus the module map's `interfaces` (so the Verifier knows the paths and entry points stages will create, which is what E103's and E135's first-try defects lacked) and the architecture's text. It writes definitions and programs for every criterion the plan's stages validate, the tier's kinds, the floor's areas, and (first phase) the post-deploy checks of TPL §7. **Its package is the phase-authoring package, distinct from the correction package** (N04): a result schema of its own (B.7) and proposal permissions for the governed `check_commands` and the check programs its obligations need, which `src/checks/guide.ts` today tells the Verifier not to change; it keeps the protected-update route and says plainly that writing a proposal grants no execution authority. The diff is a protected proposal (D1 §7.3 step 2), **classified by D3 §3.1 as the classifier computes it**, never assumed `tightening` because files were added (B07): a later phase's addition that changes an existing check's input fingerprint (default inputs covering the protected tree, D3 §3.1) is whatever the classifier says; `unclassifiable` if it touches `check_commands`, which the first phase must to name the toolchain, so **the first phase's proposal goes to the human under every authority**, and D6 says so plainly: one human act per project for the toolchain (Q9). It is applied by D1 §7.9. **Readiness is the applied protected version, not the author's run** (B07; `D6-W04`): `check_authoring` work completes when its run does (transitions as `check_correction`, K7), but the phase's **authoring readiness** (`phase_plans.authoring_ready_at`, A.3) is set only when the authorized protected version establishing the phase's initial check obligations is applied, or when an explicit pre-implementation exception is effective; a captured, rejected or unapplied proposal establishes no readiness, and stage work's `depends_on` (§5.5) waits on readiness, so no Builder builds before the checks exist (F §5.1's default). **Deferral** (`D6-W06`): a check the Verifier cannot write yet (its interface does not exist) is listed in its result as `deferred_criteria`, each with the reason, the prerequisite stage or interface, and the engine records it as an unresolved obligation with a follow-up authoring identity; the prerequisite stage's integration makes the follow-up `check_authoring` eligible **exactly once**; until the deferred checks exist and pass, the phase and deployment gates carry `AUTHORING_INCOMPLETE` naming the criteria (F §5.1: "additional checks may be introduced when interfaces become executable", not dropped). **The exception** F §5.1 allows (`build_first`, the owner's `blocker` answer on a parked authoring item) records all three of F §5.1's conditions: the owner's reason, an independent review (a Reviewer run or a second human, recorded), and a gate-visible binding to the affected scope; it releases the Builders and waives no acceptance coverage: the obligations stay and the gates stay incomplete until the checks exist.

**6.3 A stage's chain** (as built, with K3). `stage_build` eligible → Builder run (`deadline_builder`) → snapshot, validation, integration (CAS; a rebase if another stage integrated meanwhile, D1 §7.5) → `stages.status` `integrated`, `integrated_revision`, **the stage-code milestone its dependants wait for** (§6.4; B08) → nomination at T2 and T3 (`engine_cadence`), at T1 on the Builder's request or at phase completion (§5.8) → checks registered (trigger `nomination`) and run by the runner, serially per candidate (`max_concurrent_checks`) → `verification` work → Verifier run (reads the results and the code read-only, raises findings, proposes corrections) → at T2 and T3 `review` work once every required check passed (`queueReview`, as built) → Reviewer run (sign-off bound to the acceptance content hash, or findings) → `stage` gate evaluated at each write (D1 §9.5) → satisfied: `completeStageWork`, `stages.status` `verified`. Repairs: a failed required check sends the item `verifying → eligible` once per candidate (D3 §2.10); a `fix` disposition creates fix work (E43) whose integration nominates the fix candidate at every tier (E104); `repair_attempts_max` (3) and `no_progress_max` (2) park with a `blocker`. A Builder's objection stops repair and asks the owner (`correct_check`, `change_spec`, `retry`, `cancel`; D3 X2), and §5.7 adds `replan`.

**6.4 Ordering, dependencies and what runs in parallel** (Q4, decided by Sean's own answer, E139 item 4: **concurrency is dynamic, sized to the machine**; K2, K4). **Order** is the plan's, with **two dependency milestones** (B08; `D6-X04`): **stage-code readiness**, met when the referenced stage's **active generation** (§5.7: the stage as the current plan generation holds it) has integrated code on the branch and is neither `cancelled` nor parked, not when it is `verified`; and **phase-authoring readiness** (§6.2). A stage's work is eligible when both are met; `dispatchBlocker`'s present requirement that dependency work be `complete` is replaced by these milestones. A dependant already running stays subject to the current findings, supersession and gate checks; the milestone decides dispatch, nothing after. The default the Architect is told to use is a chain (stage n depends on n−1), and it declares independence explicitly. **Pipelining:** with stage n integrated, stage n+1's Builder may run while stage n's checks, Verifier and Reviewer run; a stage n finding dispositioned `fix` makes fix work that integrates after n+1's build (the rebase path), and the fix candidate supersedes; stage n+1's stage gate is evaluated on a candidate that holds both by ancestry (as built). **Parallel builds:** stages with no dependency between them build at once in separate workspaces; their integrations serialize on the integration lease, each later one rebased and re-validated (D1 §7.5).

**How many at once: the envelope decides, nothing else** (K2; B09; `D6-X06`). There is no run count: the project bound `max_concurrent_runs` (1 as built, BS §6 correction 20) **and the engine-wide `max_concurrent_runs` (2 as built, `dispatchBlocker`'s global count) both cease to impose an admission cap**; D1 §8.1 step 8 selects every eligible item in priority order (verification due → builds → replans → other), dispatching each that D2 §3.7's admission admits and holding the rest `resource_envelope`, under three rules kept from D1: one run per work item; integration serial under its lease; a session is a run and admits no one-shot beside it on its project (§2.7). What remains as bounds: domain slots (`max_concurrent_domains`), work and session exclusivity, and the envelope. **Admission preserves the accounting rule as built** (`src/store/transitions/envelope.ts::envelopeHold`): the host reserve, **plus the sum of the immutable maxima of the admitted domains** (this one included), plus the reservations of running services, plus self-test reservations, plus the **unoccupied shared verification capacity**, must fit within **`MemAvailable` plus the memory currently held by the domains included in that sum** (`Σ memory.current`): the add-back is what keeps an admitted domain's own consumption from being charged twice, once as its maximum and once as the memory it has taken from `MemAvailable`. An unreadable required observation (`MemAvailable`, a counted domain's `memory.current`) **holds** admission; it is never read as zero. The engine home's filesystem keeps `host_reserve_disk`; D2's volatile storage is charged to the domain's limit, not to free space outside it. Every domain is counted at a maximum, never at its use, so a host can never be over-committed by what a run might grow to; **the maximum is recorded at admission** (`execution_domains.memory_max`): a new `role` or `check` domain takes its project's effective profile maximum then, and a later configuration change resizes no admitted reservation. **Shared service-check capacity** (E126) is sufficient for the **largest effective `check_memory_max` among the services it must serve** when the surviving services belong to projects with different maxima, and it is reserved **exactly once, with its slot**, not once per service. `max_concurrent_domains` stays a hard cap. **What that decides on a host like this one** (12 to 16 GiB available during M4's runs, E130 item 1; about 12 GiB idle, E87 item 7), at the defaults (`host_reserve_memory` 2 GiB, `domain_memory_max` 8 GiB): one role domain needs 10 GiB, two need 18 GiB, so **at the defaults dynamic concurrency is one run at a time on this workstation, and none beside a running Alpha service** (E126 item 2: 2 + 8 + 0.5 + 8 for the service's check capacity = 18.5 GiB). The 8 GiB default is a ceiling chosen before any role domain was measured (D2 A.7), and no row has recorded a role's peak. **D6 therefore proposes, with the numerics Sean's** (E140 N1, N2): per-profile memory maxima under the engine's ceiling, `role_memory_max` (default 4 GiB) for `role` domains and `check_memory_max` (default 2 GiB) for `check` domains and the shared check capacity, each a project setting never above the engine's `domain_memory_max`, which stays 8 GiB as the ceiling and the fallback; each is part of the profile's qualification identity (§2.6, `D6-X09`); and each domain's `memory.peak` (cgroup v2) **read before the domain is removed** and recorded with the domain's identity on its termination, `unknown` when the file is gone or unreadable (N01; `D6-X08`), shown by the pace read (§7.6), so the defaults are revisited on measurements after the benchmark. **The 12 and 16 GiB examples assume an otherwise idle qualified host, sufficient disk, no additional reservations and enough domain slots** (B09): with 12 GiB available, two role runs (2 + 4 + 4 = 10 GiB) or one role run beside a running service (2 + 4 + 0.5 + 2 = 8.5 GiB); with 16 GiB, three role runs (14 GiB), which also needs the engine run cap gone (above) and `max_concurrent_domains` 4 (E140 N2; was 2) so that memory, not the slot count, is the ordinary limit. These sums describe an eligible idle host, not an unconditional admission result. **The cost, plainly:** a Builder that needs more than `role_memory_max` is killed at its limit (`resource_limit`, D2 §1.6) and its run fails `infra_error`; the exit class and `memory.peak` say so, and the project raises the setting. A smaller maximum is a trade of throughput against reliability that no measurement of a role's demand has yet informed (N01): a one-file stage in the real lanes ran under Claude Code in far less than 4 GiB by every sign, but no row has measured it; `D6-X06` and the pace read make it measured.

**Several machines** are not in M5 (E139 item 4): remote runners, the store and sandboxes across hosts; §11 class B says what they would need. **Verifier and Reviewer are never parallel on one candidate** (the review waits for the verification, as built), and a Reviewer reviewing a candidate never shares a project with a Builder integrating onto it before the review's content hash is bound (D1 §3.4 binds the hash at claim, as built, so a later integration changes nothing the Reviewer signed).

**6.5 Autonomy within a phase** (F §9.2 "autonomy may schedule and execute eligible work within role contracts"; Q3 decided (a), E139 item 3; K3). Today's `max_chained_roles` (default 1) stops after every role for a human `continue` (D1-34; `chainBoundary`). D6 replaces the counter's meaning with an **autonomy profile** per project, `autonomy: step | stage | phase`, applied by the same boundary mechanism: `step`, as today, a decision after every role run; `stage`, the chain Builder → checks → Verifier → Reviewer → repairs runs unattended and the boundary falls before the next stage's Builder; `phase`, every stage of the open plan, the phase verification and the replan run unattended, and the boundary falls only where F routes a decision: a `plan_approval` (§5.4), a spec change, a loosening or unclassifiable correction, a Medium or higher disposition the Reviewer cannot make (O3), a blocker, a parked item. `max_chained_roles` stays as a hard upper bound on **inherited chain depth** (default raised to 24 under `phase`; N02): it bounds how many runs a chain can inherit from one origin, and a fresh plan-stage trigger starts a new chain, so it is **not** a project-wide invocation budget and D6 does not call it one. What bounds the whole: the fix loop by `repair_attempts_max` and `no_progress_max`, every run by its deadline and budget, the project by its day budget (D1 §13.3); each is a separate bound, and none is represented as one aggregate chain counter. The chain cap must be shown to bind on its own, independent of a budget (`D6-X03`). **Nothing on F §9.1's floor moves:** the spec and its changes, protected changes through the update process, deployment verification, the Builder's inability to accept its own work. The benchmark (§7.7) runs at `phase`; the recommended default for a new project is `phase` too, because `step` is what made Verity's human the step that never ran (CH R13; AH §5.5).

**6.6 Repair, findings and objections across stages** (as built; D3 §2.10, §2.11, X2; E43). Unchanged in rule; two additions. **A finding against an earlier stage** found by a later stage's Verifier is scoped `lineage` and applies to every successor (D1 §9.3 input 5, as built), so the fix is one `fix` item whichever stage is current. **A stage parked** (`repair_attempts_max`, `no_progress_max`, or an upheld objection against the plan) does not stop independent stages: its dependants not yet dispatched wait, **even when the parked stage's historical integration exists** (B08: the stage-code milestone of §6.4 requires the active generation to be neither cancelled nor parked, so parking after integration closes the milestone for later dispatch, while a dependant already running continues under the gates), while the owner's `blocker` offers `retry`, `replan` (§5.7), `cancel`; the phase cannot complete while any of its stages is not `verified`.

**6.7 The phase boundary.** §5.8 then §5.6. Under `autonomy: phase` the replan is registered and dispatched with no human unless routed; under `stage` or `step` the boundary falls before the replan's dispatch. The owner can at any time open an Architect session on the project (§3.4), which takes the integration lease's place for `.surety/` writes while open.

**6.8 Alpha** (D4 §1; F §3.3, §3.10.4; B14; `D6-D01`). When the roadmap's last phase is complete, or at any phase's completion under policy, the deploy request of D4 §4.1 is made: by the owner's command (`surety deploy c-N --env alpha`, D4 Q3, the default), or by the engine under the project policy `alpha_deploy: on_phase_complete` (decision Q5, recommended (a)). **The engine enters at the request, not at the authorized-work trigger:** `on_phase_complete` creates an **idempotent engine request through the same production request path as the owner's command** (`requestDeployment`, `src/store/transitions/deploy.ts`), identified by project, the phase-completion binding (§5.8), the candidate and the environment configuration (`deployment_requests.request_key`, A.3), for the newest candidate whose `phase` gate is satisfied, after the environment is configured (D4 §3.2) and the adapter qualified (D4 §2.6), and never otherwise. That path **seals the source, derives and evaluates the authorization, and only then creates authorized deploy work** (D4 §4.1 to §4.3, J3, J4); raising a `deploy` trigger (`observeTrigger`, a generic work registration) is not the request and is not how the policy acts, because a trigger is no substitute for sealing, binding and authorization. A phase gate, or a configured and qualified environment, **alone never authorizes deployment**. **Idempotence** holds after completion too: repeated ticks, a restart and a completed prior request create no second request for the same key; a superseding candidate, a changed configuration or a new phase evaluation is a new key, revalidated under D4 before any effect; a failed attempt retains the request's outcome, and the scheduler does not retry it (D4's reconcile and retry rules govern; the policy is not an unbounded retry). Everything after the request is D4's, unchanged: `alpha_authorize` over the delivered requirement set with the floor and the post-deploy obligations (J4), the seal, the deploy, the two identity reads and the behaviour checks, `alpha_complete`, `candidate.advanced`. No approval is on F §9.1's floor for Alpha, so the automatic request lowers nothing; what it removes is one command. A mid-project Alpha is accepted against the requirements delivered so far, the rest shown pending (F §3.10.4).

## 7. Pace

**7.1 What this section claims.** Where wall-clock time and tokens go in a Surety-run build, by construction and from the few measurements that exist; what F P3 and §9.2 scale with risk; what is designed to keep a stage's engine overhead small; and the instruments M5 records so that E130 item 9's benchmark can be run. **No figure here is a prediction the acceptance plan pins**; the rows of Appendix C pin that the instruments exist and are honest. **Cache savings and throughput are unmeasured until the reports record them** (N01): nothing below says a Surety-run stage is near Verity's pace, because no comparable measurement exists.

**7.2 Where a stage's time goes** (T2, the common case), in the order of §6.3, with the mechanism and the lever:

| Step | Mechanism | Time | Lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eligible → dispatched | the tick (D1 §1.5, `tick_interval` 30 s); the envelope (D2 §3.7) | 0 to 30 s per step today; a chain of eight engine steps can idle up to 4 min | **K1:** a transition that makes work eligible, ends a run, records a result or finalizes an operation requests a tick (D1 §1.5's flag, set internally and coalesced; never a re-entry into a running tick, N02), so idle latency is the tick's duration, not its interval |
| Admission | D2 §3.7's envelope at the configured maxima (§6.4) | 0 while the host has room; otherwise the run waits for a domain to end | `role_memory_max`, `check_memory_max` against `MemAvailable`; a quiet machine; the next host (§11 class B) |
| Sandbox build and launch | the launcher, the mount plan's validation, the cgroup, the domain init (D2 §2.2, §3.2) | seconds; not yet measured apart from a run | measured by §7.6 |
| Builder's **invocation elapsed time** (launched to terminal: tools, waiting and provider time, not model computation alone, N01) | the backend, the scoped package (F §3.10.8) | **the dominant term**; M3 and M4 real runs: 20 to 70 s between ledger timestamps and 5 to 7 k billable input tokens for a one-file stage (stored observations, not role durations); a real stage is minutes to tens of minutes (Verity: fixture C 857 s whole, a different endpoint) | the package's scope; the model per role (F §9.2); the stage's size (3 to 5 stages per phase, F §3.10.1) |
| Snapshot, validation, integration | engine git (D1 §7.3, §7.5) | seconds; a rebase adds a validation | none needed |
| Checks | the runner, no model, serial per candidate (`max_concurrent_checks` 1) | the toolchain's time per check, seconds to a minute each; M4's identity reads about 100 ms | `max_concurrent_checks` per project (D3 §2.5), bounded by the envelope |
| Verifier run | model | M3 and M4: 25 to 70 s, 9 to 18 k billable in, 0.06 to 0.16 USD on trivial stages | the package carries the results and the diff, not the repository's history |
| Reviewer run (T2, T3) | model | M3: about 30 s, 9 k in, 0.06 USD on a trivial stage | T1 has none (F §5.7) |
| Gate evaluation | the engine | sub-second | none |
| Repairs | one Builder run per failed check round, bounded | the Builder's time again; M4's real run: one build, one repair in try 1 | the Verifier's package (§6.2's interfaces), so checks fail for the right reason |
| Human | none within a phase under `phase` autonomy (§6.5) | 0, or the owner's time-to-decision where routed | the profile; the queue's targets (E9) |

A T2 stage on a real project is therefore the sum of three model runs plus repairs, with the engine adding seconds and, after K1, no idle. Verity's per-stage figure is not recorded separately in the predecessor reviews (CH records whole fixtures and calendar days), so the comparison the benchmark makes is per fixture (§7.7).

**7.3 Where tokens go.** Per stage: the Builder's package (requirements, ADRs, constraints, the phase plan's stage, the interfaces of dependency modules; F §3.10.8) plus what it reads; the Verifier's (results, outputs, the diff); the Reviewer's (the diff and the hash). Per phase, once: the Verifier's check authoring and the Architect's replan. Per project, once: the three sessions, whose tokens are the owner's conversation, re-sent each turn (§2.2) with `cached_in` recorded apart. **What Surety does not spend:** planning per tick (Verity's planner thrash, 54 % of one run's tokens, about 4 M in another: AH §5.5, CH §3.5) is structurally absent, because a plan is registered once by a finalizer and replanning has four named triggers (F §3.10.7); re-bought reviews (CH incident 10) are absent, because no approval launches a run (D2 §4.1, D1 §10.5); unknown cost is never zero (C4). The ledger separates billable, cached and output tokens per run and per role (D1 §13; E16b), which is the benchmark's unit.

**7.4 Scaled to risk** (F P3, §5.7, §9.2). T1: no Reviewer; verification once per candidate, at phase completion or on the Builder's request (E11; F §5.6), so a phase's stages build back to back, **the checks register and run at the nomination phase completion makes** (§5.8; B06: an ordinary T1 integration executes no acceptance check), and one Verifier run follows; the kinds `acceptance` and `smoke` only. T2: per-stage nomination, Verifier and Reviewer; `integration` and `security_lint`. T3: per-module sign-offs, `property` and `failure_recovery`, a security review. The floor never scales down (F §5.6, §9.1). A T1 project at `phase` autonomy is the fastest Surety can run, and it is the Switchboard pace F's §1 asks for; a T3 project is slower by construction and should be.

**7.5 What is designed to keep the engine's share of a stage small** (no measurement yet, N01). (1) No human within a phase (§6.5), where Verity's stages waited for `verity review` and merge by hand or by a worker whose gates a human opened (CH §5.2, R13). (2) Event-driven ticks (K1). (3) Pipelining and parallel builds as far as the dependencies allow and the machine admits (§6.4): on this workstation at the per-profile maxima, two runs at once on an otherwise idle host; on a larger host, more, with no setting to change but the ceiling. (4) Checks without a model, serial per candidate but concurrent with runs. (5) Planning once per phase. (6) Fixed role prompts and a package stable within a phase, which gives the provider's cache the chance to serve repeated turns and runs; whether it does is the ledger's to show. (7) The Verifier's package carrying the plan's interfaces, so fewer repair rounds are spent on checks that could never pass (E103, E135 try 1). **What makes it slower than Verity by construction, and is kept:** the independent Verifier and Reviewer runs per stage at T2 (Verity ran a reviewer per stage too), the checks running before the Builder sees its result (Verity's CI ran after merge), and the engine's refusal to count anything it did not observe.

**7.6 The measurements M5 records** (D1 §12.2: projections from entities, never a second state; N01; `D6-M01`, `D6-M02`). **The pace contract declares the durable observation or event underlying each figure**, and a figure whose observation was not recorded when it occurred is `unknown`, never recomputed from the present or measured zero. Every figure below is derived from rows that exist or that K1 to K11 add, and read through `GET /v1/projects/:p/pace` (`surety pace [--json]`), with `unknown` where a row is missing:
- **Per run:** `created_at`, `claimed_at`, `launched_at` (the `launched` status observation), `finished_at`; wait (created to launched), **invocation elapsed time** (launched to the terminal observation: tools, waiting and provider time included, not model computation), end protocol (terminal to `ended`); billable, cached and output tokens, cost and its status, model observed; outcome and reason class; repair ordinal. A missing endpoint (a restart between `launched` and the terminal observation) leaves the interval `unknown` or explicitly bounded by the observations that exist.
- **Per stage:** eligible (both milestones met, an event the scheduler records, `work.dependency_met`) to `integrated`; `integrated` to the first `stage` evaluation; to `verified`; the counts of Builder, Verifier, Reviewer and fix runs; checks run and their total duration; repairs; findings by severity; **the human wait as the union of the blocked intervals** of the decisions in the stage's `blocked_while_open` that fall within the stage's measured lifetime (overlapping decisions are not summed twice; decision-hours may be shown separately as a sum).
- **Per phase:** plan registered to `completed_at`; check authoring's time and proposal class; authoring readiness; stages; phase verification; replan time and its routing (mechanical or human).
- **Per project:** `project.created` to `baseline.spec_approved`; to `baseline.architecture_approved`; to each `baseline.phase_complete`; to `candidate.advanced` `alpha_deployed`; sessions' turns, time and tokens; totals of everything above; **eligibility and hold intervals** recorded as events when they occur (`work.held` with the hold reason, `work.dispatched`): idle time is the elapsed time of those intervals by hold (`resource_envelope`, chain boundary, `depends_on`, open decision), not a count of ticks.
- **Per domain:** `memory.peak` read before the domain's removal and recorded at termination with the domain's identity, `pids` peak where read, the writable bytes used, `unknown` when unavailable; so that §6.4's defaults are revisited on measurements.
- **The host, recorded at the project's start and at each engine start** (`host_observations`, A.3): engine version, host qualification, backend entry (binary hash, model), `autonomy`, `tick_interval`, `domain_memory_max`, `role_memory_max`, `check_memory_max`, `max_concurrent_domains`, `MemAvailable` then, and the peak number of domains running at once as the scheduler observed it; a historical run is reported with the values recorded for it, never with the present configuration, so two runs are comparable (AH §7: "comparisons need the fixture commit/hash").
The read is a projection; it never changes an `observed_at` and never calls a backend (D1 §11.3). **A benchmark report** (§7.7) binds the fixture revision, the starting baseline, the endpoint, the model, the profile and the counted human and qualification time; unlike endpoints are not reported as a speedup (N01).

## 8. The second journey: an existing repository to Alpha (E6; Q8 decided (b), E139 item 8)

**8.1 The journey.** The same engine, the same sessions and the same phases as §1, entered from code instead of an idea (E6: "no new roles: Spec Writer and Architect take code as input instead of an idea"). One existing repository, not yet public (an adopted public repository needs the delivery repository, D5's; §11 class B), with its own tests and no Surety records.

1. **The owner adopts the repository:** `surety project adopt <name> --repo <path> --branch main [--idea idea-brief.md]`. The engine resolves the branch's head, unpushed commits included (E18), records it as the **adoption baseline revision A** on `projects.adoption.pinned_revision`, **immutable and recorded before the bootstrap commit** (B11; `D6-R01`: the pin is a compare-and-swap against the branch as observed, so a branch that moves between observation and bootstrap refuses the adoption rather than pinning a revision that was never the head), registers the branch as the integration branch, journals the bootstrap commit of `.surety/project.json` and, if given, `.surety/idea.md` (else an idea record saying the project is adopted and its intent is pending), and reads **size and deployment signals** as observed evidence: tracked files and bytes, commits, languages by extension, files matching test conventions (`test/`, `tests/`, `*_test.*`, `*.test.*`, `*.spec.*`), CI and deployment files (`.github/workflows/`, `Dockerfile`, `docker-compose*`, `Procfile`, `*.env*` names only). It raises `adoption_mode` (D1 A.8; E6) with those signals and a recommendation derived from a size threshold (`adoption_full_max_files`, F §9.2 "the size threshold that drives its default recommendation"). **The owner chooses `full` or `scoped`.** `project.adopted`, baseline `idea`. Environment records for anything the signals suggest is deployed start `unknown` by name with no adapter (E6: "deployments the engine did not perform start as Unknown"); existing releases, if tags name them, are recorded `external_unverified` on `releases` with no artifact (E6), never a candidate progress.
2. **A Spec Writer session in retrofit mode** (§8.2): the workspace is the repository; the role reads the code and writes `.surety/spec/spec.md` in TPL's shape with two retrofit additions: each requirement's index row carries `Origin` `observed` (the code does this today) or `intended` (the owner said so and the code does not), and TPL §12 **Claims and reality**: one row per claim found in the README, documentation or comments, what the code was observed to do, and the requirement key that records it or `none`. In `full` mode every behaviour of the code gets a requirement with criteria; in `scoped` mode the whole project's requirements are listed but criteria are written only for the modules the owner names for the first changes (E6), the others recorded as descriptive with no criteria. **Provenance is engine-owned** (B10; `D6-R03`): every requirement a retrofit-mode session produces is recorded `reverse_engineered` and unconfirmed by the engine, whatever its `Origin` column says; `Origin` is the role's claim about the code and cannot supply confirmation, so an omitted column or an `intended` label does not remove a requirement from the confirmation decision. A requirement leaves that state only by an explicit owner action on that identified requirement, or by the engine binding it to an identified owner-authored requirement already approved (an idea brief's or an earlier spec's). Close raises `spec_approval` consolidated with one `requirement_confirm` per reverse-engineered requirement (E9's inventory; D1 A.8; one batch item, each row consumed separately, D1 §10.3), **each bound to the exact proposed requirement text and criteria**. **The owner approves the spec and, per requirement, answers `confirm` (intended, keep), `defect` (the behaviour exists and is wrong: the requirement's text is the intended behaviour, its criteria will fail on the adopted code, and its delivery belongs to a later phase) or `withdraw` (not wanted).** **The mode rules are explicit** (`D6-R09`): in `full` mode the `approve` option is blocked until every in-scope reverse-engineered requirement is confirmed or withdrawn and has the required criteria (a `defect` is an explicitly recorded pending obligation, scheduled to a later phase, not an exclusion); in `scoped` mode criterion-less descriptive requirements may remain, visibly pending, and the engine refuses any plan whose stages touch their modules until the ordinary spec-change and confirmation path gives them criteria (§5.3 clause 4 over the module; `D6-R04`). Both modes classify every module's sensitivity. The generic spec-approval check applies these rules; it never silently excludes an incomplete full-baseline requirement. Effects as §3.4, plus: a confirmed requirement is `pending` like any; a `defect` one is `pending` with `origin` `observed` and `defect` true; an unanswered or criterion-less one in `scoped` mode is `descriptive_unverified` (D1 A.2), excluded from every scope's obligations and from the plan check's validation clause until it is confirmed and has criteria, shown pending in every projection with its mark (E6: "never silently promoted to a requirement"). `baseline.spec_approved`, `requirement.confirmed`.
3. **An Architect session in retrofit mode:** the architecture as it is, the **whole-project module map with sensitive-area classification in both modes** (E6: the floor applies from day one), ADRs reconstructed from the code with `reconstructed: true`, the roadmap whose **phase 1 is the conformance phase** (E6: "carries the code unchanged through the protected acceptance path, Alpha deployment and deployment verification; no change is planned until conformance is established") and whose later phases hold the owner's intended changes (`intended` requirements, `defect` ones), and `phase-1.json` with one stage marked `conformance: true` (K11): it implements **the approved conformance set**, the confirmed requirements the roadmap assigns to phase 1, and not the `defect` or `intended` ones the roadmap schedules later (B11: a conformance stage that implemented a confirmed defect would deliver it under D1 §9.1 before anything fixed it); it has no tasks, and its validation covers the set's criteria, the whole sensitivity floor and the tier's kinds. The mechanical check (§5.3) and the consolidated `architecture_approval` + `plan_approval` as §3.4. **The owner approves.**
4. **Check authoring for the conformance phase** (§8.3): the Verifier writes protected checks for the confirmed requirements' criteria, the floor and the post-deploy checks, and may **adopt** existing tests into the protected path by copying them under `.surety/checks/`; the proposal is approved as §6.2 (the first phase's `check_commands` goes to the owner, Q9).
5. **The conformance stage** (K11; B11; `D6-R05`): no Builder runs, and **a durable conformance-stage lifecycle** (A.5) stands where stage-build work stands for every other stage, in nomination, ancestry and delivery, verification registration and gate completion; the stage cannot pass from the absence of stage-build work. At the plan's publication the engine binds the stage's **application source to A** and marks it `integrated` at the integration head of the moment, nominates a candidate c-1 **whose application source is A** (E6: "that revision is the first candidate") with the effective approved protected tree beside it (D3 §2.2's candidate source plus protected tree); its checks register and run against A; `verification` and, at T2 and T3, `review` work run as §6.3; the `stage` gate is evaluated. **Conformance is equality of the adopted application-source projection** (B11), modes and paths included, between A and the candidate, **not equality of the integration HEAD to A**: the engine-owned intent and protected commits (bootstrap, spec, architecture, plan, checks) have advanced the branch, and the projection excludes `.surety/` and nothing else, defined and checked mechanically (`conformance_source_digest`, A.3). Movement of the pinned source binding, or an application-source change outside the projection's exclusions, refuses conformance (`CONFORMANCE_SOURCE_MOVED`). **A failing check here means one of two things and the engine lets the owner say which** (B12; `D6-R06`): the check is wrong (the owner confirmed the behaviour, so the Verifier corrects the check through the protected path, D3 §3, with its invalidation) or the specification is wrong (a spec change through §4, with its invalidation); a label, a disposition or a roadmap move is neither. **A `fix` disposition during conformance records an unresolved finding and the proposed future remediation; it verifies nothing and removes no obligation.** Moving the requirement to a later phase does not make its failed required check pass, does not change the conformance stage's delivered set and does not authorize Alpha; `accept` and `defer` remain under F §6.2's severity ladder and cannot override a failed required check. **If unchanged adopted code cannot satisfy the confirmed conformance baseline, conformance stays blocked and the owner sees that condition** (the gate names the check and the finding); any route that repairs source before conformance is a separate decision amending E6, not built (§12.3). No source change is made in phase 1.
6. **Phase verification, Alpha of the adopted code, and the replan** as §1 steps 8, 9 and 12: the `phase` gate over the approved conformance set; a deployment of c-1 to the owner's fresh Alpha environment (configured and qualified as D4; the engine never deploys to or reads the project's pre-existing environments, whose records stay `unknown`), verified by observation; `alpha_deployed` for the adopted code with the `defect` and `intended` requirements shown pending. **The Alpha barrier** (B11; `D6-R10`): for an adopted project only, phase 1's completion **for the purpose of releasing the change-phase replan** additionally requires `alpha_complete` satisfied for a candidate whose application source is A (E6: Alpha deployment **and deployment verification** before any change is planned); `alpha_deploy: manual` leaves the barrier closed until the owner's command deploys, and a failed, unknown or stale verification leaves it closed. Only then the Architect's one-shot replan of phase 2 from durable records.
7. **Phase 2 onward is §1's journey**, stage by stage: the owner's intended changes and the confirmed defects, with Builders, the protected checks already in force and the adopted tests as developer evidence where the Verifier left them.

**Human acts:** the adoption and mode choice (1), the spec approval with the confirmations (2), the architecture and conformance-plan approval (3), the toolchain proposal (4), the deploy command unless policy (6; under `manual` it also opens the Alpha barrier). The confirmations are the one act the first journey does not have, and E6 requires it.

**8.2 Retrofit mode for the sessions.** A context-package variant, not a role (E6): the prompt asks for description before prescription, the repository is named as the input, and the identity block, the index and the module map carry the retrofit marks (`reverse_engineered`, `Origin`, `reconstructed`). The Spec Writer's parse-at-save (§3.2) gains the `Origin` column (K10: the index parser of D3 §4.5 accepts an optional sixth column, default `intended`, so TPL's five-column index still parses; **syntax only**: in retrofit mode the confirmation decision covers every requirement the session produced whatever the column says, B10, `D6-R02`) and TPL §12's table (parsed: `claim_source`, `claim`, `observed`, `requirement`). A retrofit-mode session may read everything in the repository and write only its role's paths (§2.5), as any session; existing tests, CI files and documentation are inputs it describes, never evidence.

**8.3 The evidence rules** (F P1, §5.1, §5.2, §5.4; F §1's "no self-grading"; E6). Nothing of the adopted repository is acceptance evidence by default:
- **Existing tests are Builder-owned developer tests** (E6; F §5.4). They never satisfy a gate as they stand. A project may make their run **supporting evidence** with a `developer`-origin check definition (D3 §2.9) that runs the suite in place; such a check covers no criterion and counts toward no kind, and may be required.
- **Adoption into the protected path** is the Verifier's act through F §5.3's process, in the conformance phase's check authoring or any later one (B13; `D6-R07`): adoption identifies **the test's acceptance logic, not only its entry point**: the assertions, the expectations, the fixtures, the assertion helpers it imports and the runner configuration that decide its verdict. Those acceptance-controlling artifacts are **copied** under `.surety/checks/` (the protected root; D1 §5.2) and the copy's imports are rewritten to the copies, or the Verifier replaces them with independent protected logic; an adopted check **cannot delegate its acceptance decision** to the original mutable suite or its helpers. The copy is given a definition of origin `acceptance` naming the criteria it covers, and the proposal lists each as `adopted_tests: [{from: [paths], to, criteria, dependency_boundary: [{path, disposition: copied | replaced | application}]}]` (B.7), so the Reviewer or the owner approving the proposal sees what was copied rather than written and **where the dependency boundary lies** (D3 §3.2: the Reviewer's assessment of test adequacy is the control). The copy, not the original, is what runs and what the fingerprint covers; the original stays the Builder's, and a later change to an original helper or expectation changes no protected check's verdict. Importing the application as the subject under test (`application` on the boundary) remains subject to D3 §6 class C's limits and is not evidence of protected assertion ownership; the Verifier is told to prefer tests that run the candidate as a child. The engine's check is that nothing under the protected root resolves an import outside it except declared `application` entries (the package names the rule and the proposal records the boundary); **no general semantic dependency analyzer is claimed**.
- **Existing CI configuration and existing results** (a green badge, a prior run's log) are not read by the engine and are not evidence (CH §5.1: nine stages "done" before CI ran).
- **Existing deployments** are environment facts `unknown` (E6, F §3.6); a release the repository tags is `external_unverified`, never a candidate progress; Surety's Alpha is a fresh environment the owner configures.
- **The conformance stage's own result is the only acceptance of the adopted code**: candidate c-1's checks, by D3's runner, under protected definitions the Verifier wrote or adopted and the Reviewer or owner approved, against application source A with the effective protected tree. A failing required check keeps the gate unsatisfied (B12; §8.1 step 5): the owner's `fix` disposition records the unresolved finding and the proposed remediation and changes nothing else; it creates no fix work in phase 1 (E6: no source change until conformance), moves nothing out of the conformance stage's delivered set and leaves the check required and failing; a roadmap move of the requirement is a planning statement about a later phase, not a resolution (D3 §2.11's exact-criterion resolution rule, E90, stands). The ways out are the governed ones: a protected correction when the check is wrong, a spec change when the confirmation was wrong, each with its invalidation; `accept` and `defer` as F §6.2 under the Alpha ladder (a High in a sensitive area blocks Alpha of the adopted code as of any), never over a failed required check. Otherwise conformance remains blocked and the owner sees it. No reclassification of a confirmed requirement to `defect` happens without the owner, and a `defect` answered at confirmation (§8.1 step 2) is scheduled to a later phase before the conformance set is fixed, which is the only way a known defect leaves the conformance set.
- **Scoped mode's descriptive requirements** are `descriptive_unverified` until the phase that touches their module writes their criteria (E6: "detailed acceptance criteria per phase as changes touch modules", F §3.10.3), through a spec change (§4) proposed by the Spec Writer run that the replan requests (a `replan` result may carry `spec_change_requests` for criteria of descriptive requirements its phase touches; each is an ordinary request, §4.2, approved by the owner); **the plan check refuses a stage touching such a module until those criteria are approved** (B10; `D6-R04`, `D6-R08`), so planning and dispatch cannot proceed on the replan's voluntary request alone.

**8.4 What is different from the first journey, and nothing else.** The adoption route, the immutable source revision A and the signals; the `adoption_mode` decision; retrofit mode's prompts, marks and the two parsed additions (K10); engine-owned provenance and `requirement_confirm` consolidated with the spec approval, with the mode rules (B10); the conformance stage with no Builder, its lifecycle and its source projection (K11, B11); the conformance-phase `fix` rule as B12 states it; the Alpha barrier before the change-phase replan (B11); adopted tests' dependency boundary (B13); `external_unverified` releases. Everything else (sessions, approvals, check authoring, the stage chain, autonomy, concurrency, the phase gate, the replan, the spec change, Alpha) is §2 to §7 unchanged. **Not in M5** (§11 class B): adopting an already-public repository (delivery reconciliation from the adoption commit, F §7.6, is D5's with the delivery repository); the Mechanic's view of an adopted production; dogfooding Surety on itself (D1 §19.3), which this journey makes possible but M5 does not run.

**8.5 The real run** (Q7's plan; E139 item 8 asks for one or a stated reason why not). **One, smaller than the first:** the repository the M4 real run's Builder wrote (the dependency-free Node service, about fixture-C-sized at one module) or an equivalent small repository Sean names; `full` mode; T1 so the conformance phase needs no Reviewer; a real Spec Writer and Architect session (Opus, Q6) with Sean confirming the observed requirements; a real Verifier authoring and adopting; conformance with no Builder; Alpha of the adopted code; one phase-2 stage by a real Builder. Expected under about 3 USD in Claude Code's estimates (two sessions, three or four one-shot runs), an estimate, not a cap; Sean's time about forty minutes in the sessions. Why a run and not a reason: the confirmations and the adopted tests are judgements no scripted backend exercises, and E6's "claim versus reality" is exactly what a model gets wrong (`D6-J04`).

## 9. Boundary and security notes

Sessions change nothing of D2's boundary: a turn is a one-shot invocation in a `role` domain with the validated mount plan, the proxy as its only egress and no control-plane reach (D2 §2). What is new is the owner's typed text entering a prompt: it is the owner's own and trusted (D2 §2.1), redacted by registered patterns before it is recorded (D1 §14.2), and never a `raw_user_report` (E5). The Architect's and Spec Writer's products are committed content and therefore untrusted input to every later role (D2 §2.1: "the repository's committed content, which roles write"); the engine parses their machine files with closed schemas (Appendix B) and refuses unknown fields, as it parses check definitions (D3 §1.4). A plan is never executed; it is registered. Automatic deployment under `alpha_deploy` requests nothing a human could not request, through the same request path, and passes the same gate (D4 J3, J4; B14); the capability stays the Release Operator's (D4 §8). The autonomy profile moves no floor item (§6.5). An engine-requested tick (K1) runs the same tick (D1 §1.5), one at a time, coalesced, never re-entered, and never ahead of D1 §8.1's safety steps (N02). A session's idle generation and a plan's active generation are compare-and-swap values (B01, B08); nothing a role writes can advance either.

## 11. What D6 does not claim

In the form of `docs/acceptance/reports/M4-not-claimed.md`: class A is outside the threat model, class B is real behaviour no test has yet established or that M5 does not build, class C is a limitation accepted by design.

**Class A.** As D2 §8 class A. An owner who approves a specification he did not read, or an architecture he did not understand: the floor is his approval, and the engine shows him the parsed index and the check's record, not a judgement of their sense. A Spec Writer or Architect that writes what the owner asks for when the owner asks for something unwise.

**Class B, not built in M5.** Adoption of an already-public repository, the Mechanic's view of an adopted production, dogfooding Surety on itself (§8.4). A route that repairs adopted source before conformance is established (B12; a decision amending E6, not taken). Independent rebinding of several pending spec-change proposals (one proposal pending at a time instead, §4.2). A general semantic dependency analyzer for adopted tests (B13; the import boundary is checked, meaning is not). Sessions on Codex, or on Claude Code by API key (the real lane is the subscription template; the API-key template is qualified one-shot only until its canary runs). Provider-native session continuation (Q1 (b)). The automated architecture approval of F §9.2. Evidence reuse across a spec change (F §3.8's applicability assessment; D6 re-runs instead). A UI for sessions and the queue (E14). Beta, Live, rollback and reattachment (D5). More than one open session per project. Parallel role runs at the engine's 8 GiB default on this workstation (§6.4's arithmetic: the proposed per-profile maxima are what make two possible, and no row has yet measured a role's peak).

**Several machines** (E139 item 4): one engine, one host, is all M5 claims. Running role or check domains on a second host, or the store on one host and sandboxes on another, would need: a remote runner contract through which the engine places a domain on another host with D2's launcher, init, mount plan and launch authorization reproduced there, over an authenticated channel the engine opens (the launch socket is a local unix socket today); the repository's objects and the context package present on the remote at the run's base, and the result and provider files collected back through the secret screen; per-host admission envelopes and host qualifications, since `MemAvailable` and the cgroup hierarchy are per host; D4's identity reads made on the host that runs the service; secrets delivered to the remote init without a record; the single-writer store staying on one host (D1 §1.3), with every remote fact a read the engine makes and records with its source. Each is a design of its own, after M5.

**Class B, not yet established:** that a Spec Writer session ends in a specification an owner approves, that a retrofit-mode session describes a repository truthfully (E6's claim versus reality is the real run's to show, `D6-J04`), that an Architect's plan passes the mechanical check first time, that a real stage's Builder, Verifier and Reviewer take the times §7.2 extrapolates, that the provider's cache absorbs any of a re-sent transcript, that a role runs within `role_memory_max`, that two useful real workloads fit this host at the trial maxima, and that pipelining shortens a phase: each is a measurement §7.6 makes and the benchmark reports, not a property a test can pin (N01). The 4 GiB, 2 GiB and four-domain defaults are trial values conditional on qualified profiles and recorded peaks, not a capacity measurement.

**Class C.** **Judgement:** the mechanical plan check proves references, approvals, dependencies and coverage, never that the plan is sensible, the ADRs sound or the stages well cut; the owner's architecture approval and the Reviewer's findings are the controls, as D3 §6 says of checks. A planned criterion with a vacuous check still passes D3's classifier (D3 §6 class C). **The specification:** the engine reads TPL §5's index and the headers; prose it does not read can contradict the index, and the Spec Writer is asked to keep them consistent, not made to. A requirement marked `human-test` has no check and is never `verified` by a gate; it stays visibly pending and the owner knows it. **Sessions:** the transcript is re-sent each turn, so a long session costs more per turn as it goes, and the cache's effect is the provider's to give; the redactor catches registered forms only, so a secret the owner types in an unregistered form reaches the provider and the transcript record; an idle timeout saves and closes, and what the role said it would do next is in the transcript, not in the engine. **Pace:** §7.2's figures are from one-file stages in the real lanes; the engine's own overhead is seconds and K1 removes the idle, but the model's time is the model's. **Autonomy:** `phase` lets a project spend up to its day budget with no human; the budgets and deadlines are the stop (D1 §13.3), and a run that spends to its deadline producing nothing is a Builder's failure the repair accounting catches, not something the engine prevents. **Impact analysis** is textual over ADRs (a key cited in an ADR's text) and structural over modules, each reported as its class; exact over requirement and criterion text, checks, stages and evidence; where semantic independence is not established the scope is conservative and shown as uncertain (B05). **Ordering:** a stage integrated out of plan order by a parallel build is correct by the rebase, but the plan's `depends_on` is the Architect's word; an undeclared dependency shows as a failed check, not as a refusal. **Concurrency:** the envelope counts a domain at its maximum, so a host with room for one run at the ceiling runs one even if the run would have used a tenth; the per-profile maxima are the owner's trade between throughput and a Builder killed at its limit. **Retrofit:** an observed requirement the owner confirms is intended behaviour because he said so; the engine marks and asks, it does not know; a `defect` the owner confirms as intended becomes a requirement a check will pass against a bug, and nothing but the owner's reading prevents it (E6: "an existing defect is never silently promoted"; loudly promoted is his right). An adopted test is as vacuous as it was written; copying it under the protected root protects it from change, not from meaning nothing (D3 §6 class C). The adoption signals read names and conventions, not behaviour.

### 12.2 Readings of the foundations settled here (no decision needed; each is a reading Sean may overrule)

1. **F §3.2 "blocking questions resolved" against TPL §10 "a spec with open questions can be approved".** Read together: an open question blocks approval only when it blocks a requirement the roadmap hint (TPL §5's phase column) places in phase 1, **or when it or its requirement has no phase hint** (B04: a missing hint cannot make approval vacuously safe); other open questions are carried visibly, and the Architect may not plan a requirement whose question is open (TPL §10; §5.3 clause 1, `PLAN_QUESTION_OPEN`). §3.4 applies it as an option blocker. Astra: sound only with that guard; applied.
2. **F §3.3's Developing gate ("task plan ... architecture approved") against F §3.10.6 ("a plan that passes ... proceeds without human approval").** The first phase plan is approved with the architecture, in one consolidated item (§3.4), because F §3.3 names the task plan in the entry gate; every later plan follows §3.10.6. No human act is added that F §3.3 did not have. B02 makes the first bundle prospective and its publication atomic, so the reading is possible as well as sound.
3. **F §3.10.5 "the Architect runs in a fresh session" against E10's `session` run kind.** "Fresh session" means fresh context from durable records, which a one-shot run gives by construction (D1 §15.3); an interactive session with no owner present would idle out. The replan is a one-shot (§5.6; **decided, E139 Q2**), the initial architecture a session.
4. **F §3.10.3 and §5.1 (checks before implementation, per phase) against F §4.1's Verifier ("approved requirements + fixed candidate → acceptance checks").** Both hold: the phase's checks are authored before its stages against the plan's interfaces (§6.2), and the Verifier of each candidate verifies against that fixed candidate; checks for interfaces that do not yet exist are added when they do (F §5.1's second paragraph) as recorded obligations, never dropped, and Builders are released by the applied protected version or the three-condition exception, not by a captured proposal (B07).
5. **F §4.1 Vision and Spec Writer as two roles against SAP, which folds Vision into the Spec Writer's listen-and-reflect phases.** Two roles, two sessions, two prohibitions (§3.1, §3.2); the owner may skip Vision by creating the project with his own idea (F §3.2 "human creates or accepts"), each path with its own durable approval binding (`idea_accept` on the Vision product, the create route's record on the owner's). Nothing in F requires a Vision session.
6. **F §3.10.2 "every approved requirement is traceable to its planned phase" against §3.8's added requirement.** An added requirement has no phase until a roadmap revision places it; meanwhile it is `REQUIREMENT_UNASSIGNED` on later phase gates (§4.5), visibly pending, never silently dropped; **only an approved, complete roadmap revision that assigns it clears the reason** (Astra's note on reading 6), never a row update.
7. **E9's inventory lists "approve architecture" as not floor and F §9.2 lists it as configurable.** Human in M5; the automated policy is class B (§11).
8. **F §5.6 cadence at T1 ("once at candidate level") against E11's T1 nomination "at phase completion, or on the Builder's request".** Read: at T1 the completion of the phase's integrations and authoring obligations nominates (E11 "at phase completion" means the completion of building), **before** the verification that depends on the nomination; draft 2's sequence, which waited for verification before nominating, was circular and is replaced (B06; §5.8). No mandatory Builder nomination is added to avoid the deadlock.
9. **D4 Q3's manual deploy against E136's "the engine driving a whole multi-stage build ... to Alpha".** The command stays the default and a policy makes the request automatic (K9), **at D4's request layer** (B14); the floor has no human item for Alpha, and the policy adds no authority.
10. **E6's "no change is planned until conformance is established" against F §6.2's `fix` disposition**, which plans a fix. **Draft 2's reading was not sound** (Astra; B12): E6 does not amend F §6.2's unresolved-until-verified rule or the failure of a required check, and moving a requirement's roadmap assignment undoes no delivery. Read now: in a conformance phase `fix` records the unresolved finding and the remediation the owner intends for a later phase, and nothing else; the failed required check keeps conformance blocked; a wrong check or a wrong confirmation is corrected through its governed path with its invalidation; a known defect leaves the conformance set only by the owner's `defect` answer at confirmation, before the set is fixed (§8.1 step 2, §8.3). A repair-before-conformance route would amend E6 and is Sean's to decide (§12.3), not a reading.
11. **E6's "existing tests are Builder-owned developer tests until the Verifier adopts specific ones through §5.3" against D1 §5.2's protected root.** Read: adoption is a copy under `.surety/checks/`, since the protected fingerprint covers paths under the roots and an in-place test would stay writable by the Builder (§8.3); **necessary, not sufficient** (B13): what is copied is the acceptance logic, helpers, expectations and runner configuration, with the dependency boundary declared, and D3's class-C limits on in-process imports stand.
12. **D2 A.7's single `domain_memory_max` against E139 item 4's "as many as the host admits".** Read: the envelope's rule stands **as built, with its add-back of the accounted domains' current memory** (B09; draft 2's statement of the rule omitted it), and the per-profile maxima are what make "as many as the host admits" more than one on this host (§6.4, K2); the ceiling is unchanged; a smaller maximum is a tradeoff Sean set by number (E140), not a measurement of role demand.

### 12.3 Questions decided, and what remains

Draft 3 builds the safe outcome without it: conformance blocked, visibly, with the governed corrections (protected, spec change) as the ways out. ## Appendix B. The machine files the engine parses

Closed schemas (unknown fields refused, as D3 §1.4). Prose files beside them are not parsed.

**B.1 `.surety/idea.md` identity block** (§3.1), a fenced `yaml` block headed `surety-identity`: `name`, `one_line`, `users: []`, `non_goals: []`, `success_measures: []`, `sensitive_areas: [] | none`, `tier_recommendation: T1 | T2 | T3 | unsure`, `environments: []`.

**B.2 `.surety/spec/spec.md`:** TPL exactly; the engine reads (B04; §3.2's semantic representation): the header fields; §5's table (D3 §4.5) with the optional sixth column `Origin` (K10); in §4, each requirement as a heading `### R<n>` (or `### R<n>. <title>`) whose **statement** is the first paragraph under it, whose **criteria** are the list items beginning `R<n>.<m>` with the criterion's text being the rest of the item, whose sensitive areas are the line `**Sensitive areas:**` and whose marks are the lines `**Human test.**` (no criteria) and `**Descriptive.**` (retrofit, no criteria yet); in §6, the module table `| Module | Paths | Sensitive areas |`; in §7, each environment as a subsection with D4's fields; in §10, each open question as a list item `Q<k>` whose blocked requirements are the keys cited in the item and whose phase hint is the trailing `(phase n)` if present; and, when present, §12 "Claims and reality" as a table `| Source | Claim | Observed | Requirement |`. Nothing outside these forms is read. The template (owner's file, BS5 §15) carries these forms exactly.

**B.3 `.surety/architecture/modules.json`:** `{version: 1, modules: [{name, paths: [], sensitive_areas: [], tier_override?: T1|T2|T3, interfaces: [{name, kind: http|cli|module|file|event, path?, description}]}], mapping_from_spec: [{spec_module, to: [name]}]}`.

**B.4 `.surety/roadmap/roadmap.json`:** `{version, architecture_version, phases: [{number, goal, modules: [], requirement_ids: [], depends_on: []}]}`; `moved_requirements` on a revision.

**B.5 `.surety/phases/phase-<n>.json`:** as §3.3: `{phase, prepared_against: {spec_version, architecture_version, roadmap_version, revision}, stages: [{number, goal, tasks: [], modules: [], implements: [], adrs: [], depends_on: [], validation: {criteria: [], kinds: [], sensitive_areas: []}, integration_obligations: [], conformance?: bool}], conformance: within_baseline | uncertain, roadmap_revision?: B.4, notes?}`; a `conformance: true` stage has `tasks: []` (K11).

**B.6 `.surety/adrs/ADR-<nnn>.md`:** front matter `key`, `status: proposed | accepted | superseded`, `supersedes?`; headings Context, Decision, Consequences.

**B.7 The role results** (`/surety/out/result.json`): session turn `{reply, artifact_changed: bool, questions_for_owner?: [], done?: bool}`; Architect replan `{summary, plan: "phase-<n>", roadmap_revision?: bool, adrs_changed?: [], conformance, notes?, findings?: []}` (findings as D3 X2); Spec Writer change `{summary, changes: [{key, kind =ImpactKind, reason}]}`; Verifier **phase authoring**, a schema of its own (N04, B07, B13): `{summary, proposal: {paths: []}, governed_changes?: {check_commands: bool, programs: []}, deferred_criteria?: [{criterion, reason, prerequisite: {stage?: number, interface?: name}}], adopted_tests?: [{from: [], to, criteria: [], dependency_boundary: [{path, disposition =DependencyBoundaryDisposition}]}], findings?: []}` (§6.2, §8.3); the correction result of D3 is unchanged; Architect replan, in retrofit: `spec_change_requests?: [{keys: [], text}]` for descriptive requirements its phase touches (§8.3).
