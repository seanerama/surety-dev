# D4: the decisions waiting for Sean (prepared while Astra reviews)

**Status:** prepared by the driver, 2026-10-07, from D4 revised draft 1 (`sdlc-design-D4-deployment.md`), its brief, the probe evidence and what M2 and M3 taught (E74, E82 to E94). **Not decided.** Astra's review (`docs/reviews/D4/sdlc-design-D4-review-brief-astra.md`) may change any of these; each is revisited against her findings before it comes to Sean, one at a time, recommendation first, hidden costs said plainly. Each decision, once made, is recorded in the errata as decided by Sean; anything settled under delegation is marked provisional.

**What D4 is, in one paragraph.** Surety can take a project to a candidate whose Alpha authorization is satisfied on engine-observed evidence, and stops there: nothing has ever been deployed, and the Alpha environment in every test is a fixture. D4 designs the part that acts on the world: a **deployment adapter** with two effects (deploy, teardown) and four reads, whose receipts count for nothing until a read of the target confirms them; a **deploy operation** that consumes an authorization once, journals every attempt with a generation, rereads its preconditions just before acting, and after a crash reconciles from the target's state before any retry; **verification by observation**, where the engine reads from the host which bytes the running process was started from, runs the Verifier's post-deploy checks against the service through a link only the engine opens, and reads again; an **environment record** that keeps "last verified", "last attempted" and "observed now" apart and shows `unknown` as unknown; **secrets** that never cross an agent or a record; and a first target, **`local_service`**: a transient service under Sean's own user service manager, in a sandbox like the agents', named from the engine home's hash, torn down by the engine. Beta and Live stay for D5.

## Part 2. The corrections D4 proposes to D1, D2, D3 and the engine as built (§12.1)

These change rules already built or approved; each names its tests. **Recommendation: approve all nine**, after Astra's verdict on each. The ones that change what the kernel does today are marked.

- **J1.** The journal gains two kinds, `deploy_apply` and `teardown_apply`, whose probe is the adapter's `reconcile` and whose finalizer writes the environment's "attempted" fact and registers verification. (Deploys get the same crash discipline git writes have.)
- **J2.** *Changes the kernel.* A sandbox domain may belong to a deployment attempt (profile `service`); such a domain lives in its own service-manager unit, and a restarted engine observes it instead of killing it, as it kills agents' domains. It ends only by a teardown or the next deploy's stop step. (What makes the service outlive the engine. Astra is asked what a re-connecting engine's authority over it is.)
- **J3.** *Changes the kernel.* Outside test mode the authorization route no longer accepts a digest, configuration identity or target set from the caller: the engine derives all three from an artifact it sealed and the configuration it holds. A new request after a finished operation makes a new authorization. (Closes a route M1 left open for fixtures.)
- **J4.** *Changes the kernel.* `alpha_authorize` is not satisfied unless the deployment could be verified: a qualified adapter, an identity method, and at least one required post-deploy behaviour check in scope. (Nothing is deployed that could never be verified.)
- **J5.** "Last verified" is the environment's fact and candidate progress is the candidate's: a deployment verified after its candidate was superseded still becomes the environment's last verified deployment, and the candidate stays where it was.
- **J6.** An environment can be the subject of an out-of-band observation, with two answers, `teardown` and `acknowledge`, and no `adopt`: the engine cannot know what bytes a hand deployment came from.
- **J7.** Verification needs two identity reads, before and after the checks, with the same running instance throughout; one read never verifies.
- **J8.** An environment-bound check reaches a local target through the engine's own link, not D2's proxy (which refuses loopback by design); either way exactly the environment's targets.
- **J9.** *Changes the kernel.* Invalidation is environment-wide: every verification and record write checks the environment's current generation in its own transaction; a late result of an older generation is kept as history and never counts; the environment lease is held through verification and completion.

## Part 3. Choices D4 made that you should see (not in its question list)

- **The Release Operator is engine code, not an agent** (§8.1, brief R1). Every Alpha step is fixed by the authorization and the configuration, judgment is what the role may not supply, an agent holding deploy authority is the predecessors' most reused prohibition (R8), and agent-run effects get refused by model safeguards (E82, E86). The role name stays reserved. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **An adapter's receipt is never an outcome** (§2.3). Only a read of the target confirms an effect; a timed-out or uncertain effect is reconciled, never retried blind. **Recommendation: accept** (the lesson of incidents 8 and 9).
- **One generation runs at a time** (§9.3): each deploy stops the old service before starting the new, so Alpha has a gap. **Recommendation: accept** for Alpha; blue-green is Live's.
- **Qualification by command, not at every engine start** (§2.6), because it creates and removes units under your service manager. **Recommendation: accept.**
- **No `adopt` for a hand-changed environment** (J6); after `acknowledge` it shows `degraded` until the engine deploys again. Astra is asked whether a re-verification should be allowed to clear it.
- **What D4 does not claim** (§11), worth knowing: an application that re-executes the same runtime with the same arguments is not distinguished; a check may be meaningless and still pass (D3's class C); a same-uid process on the host can read the secret files and change the sealed artifact (detected at the next deploy, not before); `healthy` is liveness and identity, never behaviour; the service can be down between observations.

## Part 5. What M4 would then be (for orientation, not for decision yet)

Seven slices after M3's, journey first (the prompt's outline): the walking deployment on a scripted adapter (kernel); the service domain in a real user unit with the identity read and teardown (sandbox); behavioural verification through the link (sandbox, kernel); crash and recover (kernel, sandbox); the environment record and out-of-band handling; secrets and authority; then qualification by your command, the reference project's journey, the real run if Q10 is (a), service limits on `mini-hp01`, the report and a hands-on. Nothing before the last slice spends money or touches anything outside a test's own disposable home.
