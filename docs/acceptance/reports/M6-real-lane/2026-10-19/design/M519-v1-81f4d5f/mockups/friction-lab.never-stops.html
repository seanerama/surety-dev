Why it came out this way

The law

Friction can slow the block only if it is stronger than the pull of gravity down the slope: the block stops only when μ cos θ > sin θ, that is when μ > tan θ.

With your numbers

μ cos θ = 0.30 · cos 30° = 0.26sin θ = sin 30° = 0.500.26 ≤ 0.50 → a = g(μ cos θ − sin θ) < 0The block speeds up: it never stops

Why

On a 30° slope gravity pulls the block downhill harder than friction can hold it back, so instead of slowing down it keeps speeding up until it slides off the end.
