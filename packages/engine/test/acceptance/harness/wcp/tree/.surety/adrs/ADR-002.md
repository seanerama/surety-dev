# ADR-002: Kerf rule and placement algorithm

## Context

The layout must account for the material lost at each cut, place pieces automatically (first-fit, longest first) after honouring manual moves, and explain in mm why a piece does not fit. The specification's example: three 800 mm pieces need 2406 mm and so do not fit one 2400 mm board with a 3 mm blade.

## Decision

- A piece of a row with quantity q is expanded into q instances `<pieceId>#<k>`; placements and moves address instances.
- On a board with pieces p1..pk, one blade width lies between adjacent pieces; if material remains after pk, a final cut consumes `min(blade, remainder)` and the rest is the offcut. Segment lengths always sum to the board length.
- A new piece of length L fits a board already holding k pieces of total S when `S + L + k·blade ≤ boardLength`.
- Placement: manual placements first (kept only if the board exists and the piece fits), then remaining instances by length descending onto the first board in list order with room; the rest are unplaced. Within a board, cutting order is longest first.
- An unplaced instance's reason is `TOO_LONG` (longer than every board, naming the longest board) or `NO_ROOM` (for the board with the most free space: total needed = pieces + (n−1) cuts, against the board length).
- A move is checked against the target board's current pieces excluding the moved instance; a refusal states the piece length, the kerf and the space left on the board, and leaves the plan unchanged.

## Consequences

- The heuristic may not use the fewest boards (an accepted limitation).
- A manual placement invalidated by later edits silently falls back to automatic placement rather than blocking the layout.
- All of this lives in `public/js/planner.mjs` as pure functions, the single source of truth for screen, print and tests.
